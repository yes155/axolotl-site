Raising Baby Axolotls (Juveniles) to Adulthood

What Do Newly Hatched Axolotls Eat?

Newly hatched axolotl larvae eat only live baby brine shrimp for their first weeks, because they respond to movement, not smell. They ignore dead food until they are much larger.

Larvae survive on their yolk sac for the first 24 to 72 hours, then must eat. The first live food options are newly hatched brine shrimp, small daphnia, or moina, in that order of preference.

Hatch your own brine shrimp rather than buying it, because store-bought shrimp are too old, too large, and nutritionally spent. Freshly hatched shrimp carry an egg sac that makes them far more nutritious.

Remove unhatched brine shrimp eggs and shells before feeding, because the hard shells can block a larva's intestines.

How Often Do You Feed Baby Axolotls?

Feed baby axolotls once or twice a day while they are on live food, then twice daily as juveniles. Growing axolotls need daily feeding to develop properly, unlike adults that eat every two to three days.

Larvae at 20°C grow to about 1.5 cm within the first week with daily feeding. At 22°C, front legs begin at roughly 20 mm, around day 9, and hind legs appear at the end of the third week.

Partial water changes daily are required when feeding brine shrimp, because the shrimp die quickly in fresh water and foul it within hours. Daphnia, by contrast, live in the tank until eaten and need less frequent changes.

When Do Baby Axolotls Develop Legs?

Front legs start as buds at about 20 mm, which takes roughly 9 days at 22°C, and hind legs follow at the end of the third week. The timeline depends directly on temperature and feeding frequency.

The lungs develop around the same time as the hind legs, even though axolotls keep their external gills for life.

When the front legs finish developing and the hind legs are visible, the larvae become miniature versions of adults and are then called juveniles.

Once juveniles reach about 2.5 to 4 cm, they begin taking finely chopped bloodworm, small axolotl pellets, or small pieces of earthworm. Introduce each new food gradually, because it takes several days for juveniles to recognize a new food as edible.

Why Are Baby Axolotls Biting Each Other?

Baby axolotls become cannibalistic as soon as their front legs develop, biting the gills and limbs of smaller siblings. Size segregation is the only reliable prevention.

Keep larvae of similar size together, and move any animal that grows noticeably faster into its own container immediately. Bites cost the victim energy meant for growth, and a badly bitten larva dies.

Most breeders move each juvenile into its own container as soon as it has front feet, which eliminates bites entirely at the cost of more tubs.

Keep density low in shared tanks. At 25 mm, hold a maximum of 25 larvae per 45 x 20 x 25 cm tank, and thin them out as they grow.

How Do You Cull Deformed Axolotl Larvae?

Cull any larva with a visible deformity or an inability to regrow limbs by freezing it, because these animals rarely recover and they waste resources on a defective line. Axolotls have a long history of inbreeding, so defects are more common than in most species.

The humane method is freezing: place the larvae in a small container and freeze them, which puts them into a hibernation state and dulls their sense of pain before vital functions stop.

Culling is standard practice among ethical breeders and is preferable to raising deformed animals that would carry the defects into the gene pool.

When Do You Rehome Baby Axolotls?

Start finding homes for juveniles when they reach about 5 cm, before they outgrow their grow-out tanks. Do not release them into the wild, and never sell them without disclosing the parents' genetics.

By 8 to 9 cm, move juveniles into larger containers with more water, because they need roughly the same space and water quality as a small adult tank.

Disclose the parents' morphs and any known genetics to buyers, especially if there is any chance the parents were related.

If the parents were possibly inbred, find a breeder or herpetological society to take the clutch instead of raising it yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Newly hatched larvae require food within 24 to 72 hours of hatching, once their yolk sac is gone.

Frozen bloodworm works as a juvenile food once the larvae reach 2.5 to 4 cm with front legs, which takes about 8 to 10 weeks.

With daily feeding at 20°C, larvae reach about 1.5 cm in the first week and about 7 cm by 3 months.

Baby axolotls need a gentle air stone plus daily water changes, not a strong filter, because young larvae cannot handle current.
