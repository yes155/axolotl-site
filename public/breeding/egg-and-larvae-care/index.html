Axolotl Egg and Larvae Care: A Complete Guide

How Long Do Axolotl Eggs Take to Hatch?

Axolotl eggs hatch in about 15 days at 20°C (68°F), in under 14 days at 25°C (77°F), and in more than 20 days at 18°C (64°F). Temperature is the sole control over hatching speed.

Keeping eggs warmer accelerates development but shrinks the window to prepare food. Keeping them cooler buys an extra week or two to set up live food cultures.

A female lays 100 to 1,500 eggs in a single clutch, deposited individually on plants, decor, or the tank glass. Each egg sits inside a protective jelly layer.

How Many Eggs Does an Axolotl Lay?

A female axolotl lays 100 to 1,500 eggs in a single clutch, placed one at a time over one to two days. Her size and age determine the exact number.

Eggs are laid individually rather than in a mass, each attached to a surface by a jelly layer. Live plants are the preferred egg site when they are available.

How Do You Set Up a Tank for Axolotl Eggs?

Move the eggs to a separate hatching tank filled with dechlorinated water before they hatch. A small tank around 45 x 20 x 25 cm (18 x 8 x 10 in) is enough for a clutch of about 100 eggs.

Axolotl eggs are tough. Remove them by slicing the jelly attachment point with a fingernail, or move the plants the eggs are stuck to.

Keep the water temperature near 20°C (68°F) for a steady 14-to-16-day hatch, and use water that is not soft, because dissolved salts support normal embryo development.

Do Axolotl Eggs Need Special Water?

No, axolotl eggs need the same dechlorinated water as adult axolotls, with ammonia and nitrite at 0 ppm and a pH between 7 and 8.4. No medication or special salt mix is required for normal hatching.

Use water from a cycled, mature tank when possible, because it contains the beneficial bacteria that keep ammonia at zero. Do not put eggs into freshly filled, uncycled water.

Partial water changes of about 10% every few days keep the water clean without disturbing the eggs.

How Do You Prevent Fungus on Axolotl Eggs?

White, cloudy eggs are unfertilized or dead, and removing them is the main defense against fungus spreading to viable eggs. A gentle water flow over the clutch also prevents fungal growth.

Fertile eggs darken as the embryo develops, so a pearly, translucent egg that stays clear is the one to watch. Check the clutch daily and lift out every opaque white egg the day it appears.

What Do You Do When Axolotl Eggs Hatch?

Keep the larvae undisturbed for the first 24 to 72 hours after hatching, because they live on their yolk sac and need no food. Newly hatched larvae measure 10 to 13 mm (about half an inch) and stay motionless during this period.

Start feeding live baby brine shrimp as the first food once the yolk is absorbed, because larvae this small eat only live food that moves.

Lower the water level to about 3 to 4 cm before hatching so that food concentrates near the larvae and they reach it easily.

Tear the jelly coat of eggs that have not hatched once the rest of the clutch is out, because a larva left sealed inside the jelly suffocates.

How Do You Feed and Raise Axolotl Larvae?

Feed larvae once or twice daily and keep a maximum of 25 larvae per 45 x 20 x 25 cm tank. Overcrowding starves the slowest larvae and fouls the water.

The first foods in order of preference are newly hatched brine shrimp, small daphnia, and microworms. Brine shrimp and daphnia are the most nutritious; microworms alone produce slow growth.

At 20°C, larvae reach about 1.5 cm within the first week. Front legs develop at roughly 20 mm, which takes about 9 days at 22°C, and hind legs follow at the end of the third week.

When larvae reach about 2.5 to 4 cm, they begin taking finely chopped bloodworm or small axolotl pellets. Separate them by size to stop larger larvae from biting smaller ones.

Cull any larvae with obvious deformities, because poorly formed animals rarely recover and they waste resources needed to grow healthy siblings.
