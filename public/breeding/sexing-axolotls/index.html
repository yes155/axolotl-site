How to Tell Axolotl Gender (Sexing Guide)

When Can You Tell an Axolotl's Gender?

An axolotl is reliably sexable at 18 months, because until that age every juvenile looks female. Before 18 months, every axolotl shows only a small, downward-pointing cloaca.

Males develop an enlarged cloaca between 8 and 12 months old, though some late bloomers show it only at 18 months. Until 18 months, you cannot rule out male or female with certainty.

Do not trust a pet store's sexing of a young juvenile. Young axolotls are sold labeled male or female before the characteristics exist.

How Do You Tell a Male Axolotl?

A male axolotl has an enlarged cloaca that bulges outward from the sides of its body. This is the single reliable indicator of a male.

The bulge appears as a wider, swollen area just behind the back legs, best viewed from directly behind the axolotl or from above.

Males are also slimmer with a longer tail than females in most pairs, but body shape varies enough that it is never the deciding factor.

A mature male also develops small breeding tubercles on its head, tail, and limbs during the breeding season, which females do not grow.

How Do You Tell a Female Axolotl?

A female axolotl has a small cloaca that protrudes only downward, with no side bulge. Every axolotl, including every young male, shows this shape until the male's cloaca enlarges.

Females are plumper and rounder with a shorter tail than males. A gravid female carrying eggs is visibly swollen in the abdomen, and in some cases eggs are visible through her skin.

Because the female cloaca looks exactly like an immature male's cloaca, you cannot conclude female until the animal is 18 months old and the cloaca has clearly stayed small.

Do Male and Female Axolotls Look Different?

Beyond the cloaca, male and female axolotls look nearly identical, and the differences that exist are averages, not rules. Color, gill size, and head shape do not tell you the sex.

On average, males are slimmer with longer tails and slightly larger heads, while females are rounder and, in most pairs, larger overall. None of these is decisive on its own.

The only decisive difference is the cloaca: enlarged and side-bulging in a mature male, small and downward in a female.

Can a Female Cloaca Look Like a Male's?

Yes, a female's cloaca swells temporarily before a bowel movement, which produces a false male reading. The bulge disappears once she defecates.

Check the cloaca again a few hours later. If the rounded bulge persists after the animal has passed waste, it is a mature male.

Are There Early Signs of Gender in Juveniles?

One early female indicator is the oviducts visible through the translucent skin of young juveniles, but it is not definitive. Oviducts are female-specific organs that keepers mistake for intestines.

Transparency fades as juveniles grow, so this clue is only usable in the first months. Treat 18 months as the only reliable deadline for a confident answer.
