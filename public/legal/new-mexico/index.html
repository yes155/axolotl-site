Are axolotls legal in New Mexico?

Yes, axolotls are legal to own in New Mexico, but importing one from out of state requires a state permit. The New Mexico Department of Game and Fish regulates the importation of non-domesticated animals under Importation Regulation 19.35.7 NMAC, and the axolotl falls under those importation rules.

Ownership of an axolotl already in the state requires no permit. The restriction targets importation, not possession, which is why a resident can buy from an in-state breeder with no paperwork but cannot ship one in.

Do I need a permit to import an axolotl into New Mexico?

Yes, importing an axolotl into New Mexico requires an importation permit issued by the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish. Under 19.35.7 NMAC, importing a non-domesticated animal generally requires a permit unless the species falls into an exempt classification, and the axolotl is not exempt.

The permit is not a last-minute form. Applicants apply well ahead of the planned import, because the department reviews the full application package before any animal crosses the border.

What does the New Mexico importation permit require?

The New Mexico importation process requires a containment plan, health documentation, city or county approval, and proof that any necessary federal permits have been obtained. The containment plan shows how the animal will be housed so it cannot escape, because the state's goal is keeping non-native species out of the environment.

Permit componentPurpose
Containment planShows escape-proof housing
Veterinary health certificateDocuments the animal's health
Applicable federal documentationConfirms any federal requirements that apply to the route or specimen
Disease-free documentationProves the animal carries no listed disease
County or municipal authorizationSatisfies local approval requirements

The application package is reviewed by the Department of Game and Fish before importation. This is an approval process, not a rubber stamp, so incomplete applications get returned.

How much does a New Mexico importation permit cost?

A New Mexico axolotl importation permit costs $20 for a one-time import, $25 for a Class 1 permit (1-5 animals), $75 for Class 2 (6-99 animals), and $300 for Class 3 (100 or more animals). The fee tier depends on the number of animals in the importation, so a single pet axolotl falls in the $20 one-time or $25 Class 1 category.

Fees are paid to the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish with the application. Apply at least four weeks before the planned import date so the review completes in time.

Do I need a permit to own an axolotl already in New Mexico?

No, keeping an axolotl that is already in New Mexico requires no state permit. The permit requirement applies to importation into the state, so a resident who buys from an in-state source keeps the animal under ordinary pet-ownership rules.

An in-state purchase avoids New Mexico's importation-permit process. The 2025 federal injurious-wildlife listing does not generally prohibit transport between states within the continental United States, so an out-of-state purchase still turns on New Mexico's import rules rather than a blanket federal interstate ban.

Does federal law affect axolotls in New Mexico?

Yes, but the 2025 federal injurious-wildlife listing does not generally ban transport between states within the continental United States. It restricts import into the United States and transport between the continental United States and certain other U.S. jurisdictions. For a shipment from another continental U.S. state into New Mexico, the state importation rules remain the key permit requirement.

International imports and movements involving Hawaii, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, or U.S. territories can trigger separate federal restrictions, so confirm the route with the relevant authorities before arranging transport.

Is it legal to release an axolotl in New Mexico?

No, releasing an axolotl into the wild is illegal in New Mexico and harmful to the state's ecosystems. New Mexico regulates non-domesticated animals to prevent escaped or released species from establishing in the environment, and the axolotl is non-native, so it has no natural place in the state.

A captive axolotl does not survive in New Mexico's waterways. If you can no longer care for an axolotl, find a new owner or a rehoming group rather than releasing the animal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy an axolotl online and have it shipped to New Mexico?

You may arrange an out-of-state purchase only if you satisfy New Mexico's current importation requirements. The 2025 federal injurious-wildlife listing does not itself ban transport between continental U.S. states, but international or other enumerated-jurisdiction movements can be federally restricted.

What documents do I need for the permit?

The application can require a containment plan, veterinary or disease documentation, and any federal or local approvals that apply. Confirm the current checklist with the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish before filing.

Where do I apply for the importation permit?

Apply through the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish, which issues importation permits under 19.35.7 NMAC. Contact the department directly for the current application forms and fee schedule.

Are there alternatives to importing an axolotl?

Yes. Buying from an in-state breeder avoids New Mexico's importation-permit process. If buying out of state, confirm the current state permit requirements before shipment.

This page is a plain-English summary and not legal advice. Confirm current requirements with the New Mexico Department of Game and Fish before importing any animal.
