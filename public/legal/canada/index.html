Are Axolotls Legal in Canada?

Canada allows axolotls as pets in most provinces, but federal import rules and 4 provincial permit requirements change how you buy one. This guide covers the federal law, the provincial rules, and the permit steps.

Is It Legal to Own an Axolotl in Canada?

Yes. Axolotls are legal to own in most Canadian provinces, but every axolotl entering Canada requires a federal import permit from Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC). The axolotl is also listed in Appendix II of CITES, so international shipments require CITES documentation from the exporting country. Buying a captive-bred axolotl from a Canadian breeder avoids both permit layers.

What Is the Federal Import Rule for Axolotls in Canada?

Since May 31, 2017, Canada prohibits importing all species of the order Caudata - salamanders, newts, mudpuppies, and axolotls - without a permit from ECCC. The Wild Animal and Plant Trade Regulations (SOR/96-263) list Caudata as species harmful to Canadian ecosystems. The rule protects native Canadian salamanders from the chytrid fungus Batrachochytrium salamandrivorans (Bsal). Each permit application is assessed case by case, considering origin, captive-bred status, disease status, and purpose.

Which Provinces Require a Permit to Own an Axolotl?

British Columbia, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, and Prince Edward Island require a provincial permit to own an axolotl, and the other 9 provinces and 3 territories do not. The federal rule governs import, and provincial rules govern ownership. Table by province:

Why Do Some Provinces Require Permits?

British Columbia, New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, and Prince Edward Island treat the axolotl as exotic wildlife, which requires a permit to own, import, or keep. In British Columbia, the axolotl is wildlife under the BC Wildlife Act, and the province does not grant permits to individuals for pets. New Brunswick regulates axolotls under its Exotic Animals Act, which makes owning, importing, or trafficking an exotic animal without a permit illegal. Keepers in these provinces have had axolotls seized, and pet stores fined, for selling without permits.

Is It Legal to Own an Axolotl in Quebec?

Yes. Quebec allows keeping, breeding, and selling axolotls without a permit under its Regulation respecting animals in captivity, in force since August 2022. Residents can bring one in from another Canadian province as a personal pet without special notice. Ontario and the other no-permit provinces work the same way: buy captive-bred from a Canadian breeder and keep it.

Can I Bring an Axolotl into Canada from the United States?

You can bring an axolotl into Canada from the United States only with an ECCC import permit plus CITES documentation from the exporting country. Without both, federal law prohibits the import. Border officers hold and seize specimens without the proper permits, and permits are never issued retroactively.

Can I Sell an Axolotl I Bred in Canada?

Selling within Canada requires provincial or territorial authorization in some provinces, so check your provincial wildlife department first. In provinces that require permits, selling without one is a violation. The federal import rule does not govern domestic sales.

Does Canada Ban Axolotls Because of the Fungus Risk?

No. Canada does not ban owning axolotls, but it requires a federal import permit for all salamanders to keep the fungus Bsal out of Canadian ecosystems. The restriction covers import, not domestic ownership. Axolotls bred in Canada are exempt from the import permit.

This page is a plain-English summary and is not legal advice. Confirm the current rules with Environment and Climate Change Canada and your provincial wildlife authority before importing or purchasing an axolotl.
