Are Axolotls Legal in California?

California treats the axolotl as a restricted species, and the California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW) does not issue permits for pets. This guide explains the law, who qualifies, and how the 2025 federal rule changes the picture.

Is It Legal to Own an Axolotl in California?

No. Owning an axolotl as a pet in California is not legal in practice, because possession requires a CDFW Restricted Species Permit and CDFW does not issue these permits for pet ownership. CDFW states plainly that permits are not issued to import or possess any wild animal for pet purposes. The authority is California Fish and Game Code Section 2118, which makes it unlawful to import, transport, or possess any restricted wild animal except under a revocable, nontransferable permit.

Importing an axolotlPermit required
Transporting an axolotlPermit required
Possessing an axolotlPermit required
Selling an axolotlNot authorized without a permit
Keeping one as a petNot allowed for hobbyists

California cities and counties add their own animal-keeping rules on top of state law, which makes local rules stricter in many areas.

Why Are Axolotls Restricted in California?

California classifies the axolotl as a detrimental animal, because the genus Ambystoma appears on the restricted species list in Section 671(c), Title 14, of the California Code of Regulations (14 CCR 671). Detrimental animals are species that pose a threat to native wildlife, the agricultural interests of the state, or public health and safety. Fish and Game Code Section 2118 is the statute that requires the permit.

Who Can Get a Restricted Species Permit in California?

CDFW issues Restricted Species Permits for 5 professional purposes under 14 CCR 671.1: research, aquaculture, AZA-accredited zoos, native species exhibiting, and broker-dealer trade. None of these covers pet ownership. Applicants must be California residents, at least 18 years old, with the equivalent of 2 years of full-time hands-on experience caring for restricted species.

  • Research permits for universities, colleges, and government research agencies
  • Aquaculture permits for registered aquaculturists
  • AZA permits for accredited zoos and aquariums
  • Native species exhibiting permits for licensed exhibitors
  • Broker or dealer permits for legitimate commercial trade

A new Restricted Species Permit application costs $155.53, a renewal costs $80.60, and the required enclosure inspection costs $319.50 for 1-5 enclosures up to $4,234.50 for more than 100 enclosures.

Does Federal Law Also Restrict Axolotls?

Yes, but the federal rule does not ban transport between states within the continental United States. Since January 25, 2025, the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service has listed the genus Ambystoma as injurious wildlife under the Lacey Act. The rule prohibits import into the United States and transport between the continental United States and jurisdictions such as Hawaii, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, and U.S. territories. California's own restricted-species law is what prevents ordinary pet owners from bringing or keeping an axolotl in the state.

What Happens If You Keep an Axolotl Without a Permit in California?

California game wardens cite and seize restricted animals kept without a permit, and the animal is rehomed or placed with a permitted facility rather than returned. California-based pet stores and online sellers do not ship axolotls, because selling without a permit is unlawful. Reputable out-of-state breeders refuse to ship into California.

Can I Buy an Axolotl and Keep It If It Was Already in California?

No. If you do not hold a permit at the time you acquired the axolotl, possession is unlawful, because the restricted species law applies to possession, not just purchase. CDFW does not issue permits for pet purposes. Keeping one acquired before the rule still requires a permit you cannot get.

Is It Illegal to Bring an Axolotl into California from Another State?

Yes. Importing an axolotl into California without the required state authorization violates California law. The 2025 federal injurious-wildlife listing does not itself prohibit transport between states within the continental United States, but it does restrict international import and transport between the continental United States and certain other U.S. jurisdictions. For a California pet owner, the state restriction is enough to make an ordinary out-of-state purchase unlawful.

Are Axolotls Illegal in Los Angeles or San Francisco?

State law already restricts axolotls, and many California cities and counties add their own animal-keeping rules on top, so local possession rules are often stricter. Check your city and county ordinances before acting. CDFW permits do not cover pet ownership anywhere in the state.

This page is a plain-English summary and is not legal advice. Confirm the current rules with the California Department of Fish and Wildlife or an attorney before acting.
