Are axolotls legal in Virginia?

Yes, axolotls are legal to own in Virginia, and no state permit is required. The Virginia Department of Wildlife Resources states that, as of August 1, 2021, it is legal to import, export, sell, and possess the Mexican axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum) in Virginia, with no permit required.

Virginia is one of the more permissive axolotl states. The law is explicit rather than accidental, because the axolotl was written into the regulations as an allowed species after the state removed it from its restricted list.

When did axolotls become legal in Virginia?

Axolotls became legal in Virginia on August 1, 2021, when the amended regulations took effect. Before that date, the axolotl appeared on Virginia's list of predatory and undesirable species under 4VAC15-30-40, a listing the state later called inadvertent.

The Virginia Register of Regulations records the removal. The final regulation, adopted June 21, 2021, struck the Mexican axolotl from the restricted table and took effect August 1, 2021.

What does Virginia law say about axolotls?

Virginia's regulations at 4VAC15-30-40 make the Mexican axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum) lawful to import, export, sell, and possess, with no permit required. The restricted-species table lists all mole salamanders except Ambystoma mexicanum, which means every other salamander in the family needs a special permit, but the axolotl does not.

The rule is administered by the Virginia Department of Wildlife Resources. Because the axolotl is nonnative, the one blanket prohibition that remains is release into the wild.

What is 4VAC15-30-40?

4VAC15-30-40 is the Virginia regulation on importation, possession, and sale of nonnative animals, and it now exempts Ambystoma mexicanum from its permit requirement. The regulation lists predatory and undesirable species that need a special permit, and the table's amphibian entry reads: all species of the family Ambystomatidae except the Mexican axolotl.

The exemption is the reason Virginia axolotl owners need no paperwork. The axolotl's 2013 listing as a restricted species was removed as inadvertent, and the amended text took effect August 1, 2021.

Do I need a permit to own an axolotl in Virginia?

No, owning an axolotl in Virginia requires no state permit. The state's regulations explicitly allow possession of Ambystoma mexicanum without a permit, so a resident can buy one from an in-state breeder or store and keep it without registering the animal.

Local ordinances remain the only caveat. A city or county may impose its own rules on exotic pets, so check local codes before making the purchase.

Is it legal to breed and sell axolotls in Virginia?

Yes, breeding and selling axolotls is legal in Virginia, because the regulations authorize the import, export, and sale of Ambystoma mexicanum. A Virginia breeder can sell axolotls to in-state buyers without a state wildlife permit, subject to any local business ordinances.

The federal injurious-wildlife listing does not generally prohibit transport between states within the continental United States. Virginia's state rules therefore remain central for in-state possession and sale, while international imports and movements involving other enumerated U.S. jurisdictions can trigger federal restrictions.

Does federal law affect axolotls in Virginia?

Yes, but the federal rule does not generally prohibit transport between states within the continental United States. Since January 25, 2025, the genus Ambystoma has been listed as injurious wildlife under the Lacey Act. The rule restricts import into the United States and transport between the continental United States and certain other U.S. jurisdictions.

For a shipment from another continental U.S. state into Virginia, federal injurious-wildlife law does not create a blanket interstate ban. Virginia's own rules and any applicable origin-state requirements still need to be checked before transport.

Is it legal to release an axolotl in Virginia?

No, releasing an axolotl into the wild is illegal in Virginia, because releasing nonnative species damages native fish and wildlife. The axolotl is not native to Virginia, so a released animal can compete with or spread disease to native salamanders.

If you can no longer keep an axolotl, rehome it to another keeper in Virginia. Never release the animal into a waterway.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I have an axolotl shipped to Virginia from another state?

Virginia state law allows axolotls, and the 2025 federal injurious-wildlife listing does not generally prohibit transport between states within the continental United States. Check the origin state's rules, Virginia's current rules, and the route before arranging shipment.

Are axolotls legal in all of Virginia?

Yes, the state regulations apply uniformly across Virginia, so possession is legal in every county and city unless a local ordinance says otherwise. Confirm local codes before purchase.

Is it legal to take an axolotl from the wild in Virginia?

No, and it is irrelevant, because no wild axolotl population exists in Virginia, and releasing the species into the wild is illegal. Captive-bred animals are the only legal source.

Do I need a federal permit to keep an axolotl in Virginia?

No federal injurious-wildlife permit is required merely to possess an axolotl in Virginia, and such a permit is not generally required for transport between continental U.S. states. Federal restrictions still apply to international import and movements between the continental United States and certain other U.S. jurisdictions.

Where can I buy an axolotl in Virginia?

You can buy from an in-state seller or potentially from an out-of-state seller if the shipment complies with the laws of the origin and destination states. The 2025 federal listing does not itself create a blanket ban on transport between continental U.S. states.

This page is a plain-English summary and not legal advice. Confirm current rules with the Virginia Department of Wildlife Resources before acting.
