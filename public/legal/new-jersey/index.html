Are axolotls legal in New Jersey?

No, axolotls are illegal to keep as pets in New Jersey. The New Jersey Division of Fish and Wildlife lists axolotls among the species that may not be kept as pets or for hobby purposes and may not be sold. The ban covers possession, buying, selling, importing, and transporting the species anywhere in the state.

Because the state classifies the axolotl as a restricted species, no legal private-ownership path exists. A resident cannot buy one from a store, receive one as a gift, or have one shipped in from another state.

What law bans axolotls in New Jersey?

The possession ban comes from New Jersey's restricted species rules at N.J.A.C. 7:25-4, which list axolotls, larval salamanders, water dogs, and mudpuppies as animals that may not be kept as pets or sold. The state's possession law, P.L.2023, c.32, requires a New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection (NJDEP) permit for live exotic animals, and the Division of Fish and Wildlife does not issue pet-keeping permits for restricted salamanders.

The official restricted-species document from the NJDEP names the axolotl directly. It may not be kept as a pet or for hobby purposes for any reason, and it may not be sold.

Why are axolotls restricted in New Jersey?

New Jersey restricts axolotls because the species poses a hybridization and ecosystem risk to native salamanders. The axolotl is a non-native salamander that can interbreed with the state's native tiger salamanders, and escaped or released animals could compete with local species.

Under N.J.S.A. 23:4-63.3, the Fish and Game Council designates an exotic animal as potentially dangerous when it threatens indigenous animals, the environment, agriculture, or public health. Possession of such a species requires a permit the Division does not issue for pets.

The restriction applies no matter how the animal was acquired. If a resident obtains an axolotl through any channel, possession itself violates state law.

Can I own an axolotl in New Jersey with a permit?

No, New Jersey offers no permit path for keeping an axolotl as a pet. The Individual Hobby permit from the NJDEP covers regulated exotic and nongame wildlife, but the axolotl sits on the restricted list, not the permit list, so an application for this species gets denied.

The hobby permit fee of $10 (or $20 with birds) does not change the outcome. A permit fee buys nothing for a species the rules forbid, because no permit type covers the axolotl.

What are the penalties for keeping an axolotl in New Jersey?

An illegally kept axolotl in New Jersey is subject to confiscation, and the owner faces fines, because possession without a permit violates state exotic-animal law. The Division of Fish and Wildlife Bureau of Law Enforcement enforces the restricted-species rules statewide.

If you own an axolotl, contact the NJDEP Division of Fish and Wildlife at 609-292-9591 before taking any step. Do not sell or rehome it inside New Jersey, because that transfer also violates the rules.

What happens if I already own an axolotl in New Jersey?

If you already keep an axolotl in New Jersey, contact the New Jersey Division of Fish and Wildlife for guidance, because selling or rehoming it inside the state also violates the restricted-species rules. The legal options include placing the animal with a permitted facility or moving it to a state where axolotls are allowed.

Releasing the animal is not an option. Releasing any exotic animal into New Jersey's environment without a permit violates N.J.S.A. 23:4-63.3 and harms the ecosystem.

Does federal law affect axolotls in New Jersey?

Yes, federal law bans axolotl shipments into New Jersey, because the genus Ambystoma became injurious wildlife under the Lacey Act on January 25, 2025. The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service final rule (90 FR 1898) prohibits importing axolotls into the United States and shipping them across state lines except under a federal permit for scientific, educational, medical, or zoological purposes.

The federal rule makes the state ban effectively airtight. Even if New Jersey relaxed its rules, moving axolotls across state lines remains federally prohibited.

What can I do instead of keeping an axolotl in New Jersey?

Choose an exotic pet from New Jersey's exempt-species list, because the restricted-species list blocks most aquatic salamanders. Exempt species need no permit and include budgerigars, cockatiels, canaries, zebra finches, and common iguanas, and freshwater fish are unrestricted.

Confirm any candidate on the NJDEP exempt-species and restricted-species lists before buying. Those two lists define what is legal to keep in New Jersey.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy an axolotl from a pet store in New Jersey?

No, selling axolotls is prohibited in New Jersey, so pet stores and breeders in the state do not sell them. The official restricted-species list bars the sale of axolotls for any reason.

Can I have an axolotl shipped to New Jersey from another state?

No, shipping an axolotl into New Jersey violates state restricted-species law, and since January 25, 2025, it also violates the federal injurious-wildlife rule. The shipment itself is the offense, before the animal even arrives.

Are axolotls legal in states near New Jersey?

Yes, several neighboring states allow axolotls, but the federal interstate-shipping ban applies everywhere, so an out-of-state purchase cannot be shipped to you. Legal status varies by state, so confirm the destination state's rules before planning any move.

Is the New Jersey ban new?

No, New Jersey has long restricted larval salamanders, and the current rules trace to the exotic-wildlife regulations at N.J.A.C. 7:25-4. The possession statute N.J.S.A. 23:4-63.3 continues to require an NJDEP permit for exotic animals.

This page is a plain-English summary and not legal advice. Contact the New Jersey Division of Fish and Wildlife to confirm current law before taking any action.
