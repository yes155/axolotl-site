How Often Should You Feed an Axolotl?

Hatchlings eat 2–3 times a day, juveniles eat 1–3 times a day, and adults eat once every 2–3 days. Feeding frequency drops as an axolotl grows from hatchling to adult, tracking size more reliably than age in months, since individual growth rates vary (see the Axolotl Care Guide for the full husbandry basics behind this schedule).

Planning a fast? If you are asking how long an axolotl can go without food (vacation or planned fasting), the fasting and vacation guide has the single duration reference. This page covers the normal lifecycle feeding schedule.

Three life stages set the baseline: hatchling (newly hatched to 7.5cm/3in), juvenile (7.5cm to roughly 19cm/7.5in, up to about a year old), and adult (over 19cm/7.5in, fully grown). Each stage gets its own frequency and portion guidance in the table below.

Why Does Feeding Frequency Change as an Axolotl Grows?

Feeding frequency drops because growth rate and metabolic demand fall sharply once an axolotl reaches full size. Hatchlings convert food into new tissue almost continuously, so small meals get processed fast and need replacing several times a day. Adults that have stopped lengthening redirect far less energy into tissue building, so a 2–3 day gap between meals matches actual energy use rather than starving them.

Matching frequency to life stage keeps three outcomes in check: stable metabolism, water quality, and gill and skin health. Overfeeding an adult on a hatchling's schedule adds uneaten protein to the tank, raises ammonia, and stresses the gills.

Axolotl Feeding Schedule by Life Stage and Size

Hatchlings under 7.5cm (3in) eat 2–3 times daily, juveniles between 7.5cm and 19cm (3–7.5in) eat 1–3 times daily, and adults over 19cm (7.5in) eat every 2–3 days. The table below reconciles centimeters and inches across all three stages.

Hatchling/larva0–8 weeksUnder 7.5cmUnder 3in2–3x dailyBaby brine shrimp, daphnia, microworms
Juvenile2 months–1 year7.5–19cm3–7.5in1–3x dailyChopped earthworms, sinking pellets
Adult1+ yearsOver 19cmOver 7.5inEvery 2–3 daysEarthworms, pellets

Hatchlings under 7.5cm can't yet manage earthworm pieces or pellets, so live food sized to their mouth carries them through the fastest-growth window. A juvenile that crosses the 7.5cm mark takes chopped earthworms and axolotl-formulated pellets, with daily feeding continuing until growth slows near the one-year mark.

How Much Should an Axolotl Eat at Each Meal?

One meal matches the width of the axolotl's head and finishes within 3 to 5 minutes. Three checks confirm correct portioning:

  • Head-width rule: a single earthworm piece or one to two pellets, sized to the axolotl's head, prevents choking and impaction.
  • Time-to-consume rule: food still sitting in the tank after 5 minutes gets removed rather than left for a second attempt.
  • Body-condition check: an abdomen the same width as the head signals correct feeding; a narrower abdomen signals underfeeding, and a wider one signals overfeeding.

A 40-gallon tank holding one adult axolotl eating 1–2 medium earthworms every 2–3 days typically needs a 50% water change weekly to keep nitrate under 20ppm — the portion size and the maintenance schedule scale together.

What Should You Feed an Axolotl, and How Often?

Earthworms and axolotl-formulated sinking pellets work as daily-use staples, while frozen bloodworms work only as an occasional treat.

  • Earthworms: chopped to head-width, suitable at every life stage, fed at the stage's set frequency.
  • Sinking pellets: formulated specifically for axolotls rather than generic fish pellets, fed at the same frequency as earthworms (see Best Axolotl Pellets for brand-specific comparisons).
  • Frozen bloodworms: high in protein but nutritionally incomplete alone, offered once or twice a week as a supplement, not the entire diet.

Four food types stay off the menu at any life stage: tubifex worms, hard-shelled insects, feeder fish, and high-oil live foods such as whiteworms. Tubifex worms carry parasite and bacterial risk and aren't nutritionally balanced as a sole food source. Hard-shelled insects such as crickets and mealworms resist an axolotl's digestive enzymes and cause impaction. Feeder fish introduce parasites picked up from the tanks they're farmed in.

Whiteworms and other high-oil live foods raise the risk of liver problems if fed as a steady diet rather than an occasional treat.

What Are the Signs of Overfeeding or Underfeeding an Axolotl?

Bloating, floating, and vomiting signal overfeeding; visible thinness and reduced activity signal underfeeding.

Bloated abdomen wider than the headAbdomen narrower than the head
Floating at the surface instead of resting on the substrateReduced activity and slow response to food
Vomiting within an hour of a mealThinning at the base of the tail
Absence of a bowel movement for 10+ days—

An axolotl that hasn't passed waste in 10 days needs feeding paused and water parameters checked — ammonia and nitrite at 0ppm, nitrate under 20ppm — before any other intervention (full troubleshooting in Signs of Axolotl Overfeeding).

How Long Can an Axolotl Go Without Food?

A healthy adult axolotl tolerates up to 2 weeks without food; a hatchling tolerates only 1–2 days.

Life StageEstimated Safe Fasting PeriodNormal Feeding Frequency
Larva (0–2 weeks old)1–2 days2–3x daily
Young juvenile (2–8 weeks old)3–4 daysDaily
Older juvenile (2–6 months old)5–7 daysEvery other day
Subadult (6–12 months old)10–14 days3–4x weekly
Adult (1+ years old)Up to 2 weeksEvery 2–3 days

A 2024 veterinary study at a teaching hospital used a 3-day pre-procedure fast as standard practice for healthy adult axolotls, confirming a short fast carries no clinical concern. The hatchling-through-subadult figures above scale down from that adult baseline by growth rate; only the adult ceiling is independently corroborated across multiple sources, so treat the younger-stage numbers as a conservative planning estimate rather than a validated figure.

Surviving a fast and thriving through one are different outcomes — an adult that fasts the full 2 weeks loses muscle mass and immune strength without dying.

A trip under 3 days needs no feeding adjustment for an adult; a trip over 5 days calls for a sitter who feeds on the normal every-2–3-day schedule rather than relying on fasting tolerance as a substitute (see Axolotl Vacation Care for trip-specific packing and transport steps).

Does Water Temperature Affect Feeding Frequency?

Water below 18°C (64°F) slows an axolotl's metabolism enough to extend the safe gap between meals, while water above 24°C (75°F) raises stress and can shorten survival to hours rather than weeks.

Axolotls kept at the recommended 15–20°C (60–68°F) range digest more slowly than they would at room temperature, which is part of why the every-2–3-day adult schedule works without causing weight loss (the full metabolism-and-ammonia relationship is covered in How Water Temperature Affects Axolotls).

How Do You Get Food to an Axolotl?

Feeding tongs or a dedicated feeding dish deliver food directly to an axolotl without substrate contamination.

  • Load the tongs or dish with a head-width portion of earthworm or pellets.
  • Lower the food slowly in front of the axolotl until it strikes.
  • Wait 3–5 minutes, then remove anything left uneaten.
  • Check ammonia and nitrite levels weekly to catch fouling from missed leftovers early.

Removing uneaten food within minutes keeps ammonia at 0ppm and nitrate under 20ppm, the same water-quality thresholds covered in Axolotl Tank Setup.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do axolotls need feeding every day?

Only hatchlings and young juveniles need daily feeding; adults need feeding only every 2–3 days. A hatchling under 7.5cm eats 2–3 times a day, a juvenile between 7.5cm and 19cm eats daily, and an adult over 19cm switches to the every-2–3-day rhythm once growth slows near the one-year mark.

How long can an axolotl go without food?

An adult axolotl tolerates up to 2 weeks without food, while a hatchling tolerates only 1–2 days. Survival and health aren't the same outcome — an adult that fasts the full 2 weeks loses muscle mass and immune strength without dying, so the safe maximum isn't a target to plan around.

How do you know if you're feeding your axolotl enough?

An abdomen the same width as the head signals correct feeding. A narrower abdomen than the head points to underfeeding, paired with reduced activity and a thinning tail base. Checking this weekly catches a feeding gap before it becomes a larger health problem.

How can you tell when an axolotl is hungry?

An axolotl swims toward movement at the water's surface or tank glass when hungry. Axolotls track food by water movement and scent more than sight, so an animal that ignores a moving feeding tong has already eaten enough for that period.

What is an axolotl's favorite food?

Bloodworms and brine shrimp rank as the most universally accepted foods across axolotl life stages, per veterinary feeding guidance. Individual preference still varies — some axolotls favor earthworms or pellets instead — so a mix of two or three accepted foods works better than relying on one.

What foods are toxic to axolotls?

Tubifex worms, hard-shelled insects, feeder fish, and high-oil live foods such as whiteworms are unsafe as a steady diet for axolotls. Tubifex worms carry parasite and bacterial risk, hard-shelled insects such as crickets cause impaction, feeder fish introduce parasites, and whiteworms raise the risk of liver problems when fed too often.

Can axolotls smell their food?

Axolotls detect food primarily through water-borne scent and lateral-line movement detection, not eyesight. Their eyes register light and motion but provide limited image detail, so motionless food often goes unnoticed until it drifts or an owner moves it with tongs.

Is axolotl poop toxic?

Axolotl waste isn't toxic to the axolotl itself, but it raises ammonia in the tank if left to decompose. Ammonia above 0ppm stresses gills and skin, so removing waste during routine tank maintenance protects water quality the same way removing uneaten food does.

How often do axolotls poop?

A healthy axolotl on a normal feeding schedule passes waste every 1–3 days. No bowel movement for 10 days or longer, especially paired with floating or a bloated abdomen, signals possible constipation and calls for a water-parameter check before any other intervention.

Can two axolotls be fed on the same schedule in one tank?

Two axolotls of the same life stage and similar size can share one feeding schedule. Axolotls of different sizes housed together compete for food unevenly, with the larger animal outcompeting the smaller one, so same-tank feeding works only when both are close in size and stage.
