Axolotl Behavior Explained

What is normal axolotl behavior?

A healthy axolotl spends most of the day resting on the tank bottom, flicking its gills, and moving slowly. Resting is the default state for this ambush predator, not a symptom of illness. The animal also walks across the substrate on its four legs, drifts with a slow eel-like ripple, and surfaces occasionally to gulp air.

If the movement is slow and steady, the behavior is normal. Fast, frantic, or repeated movement is the first departure from that baseline.

Which behaviors are completely normal?

Six daily behaviors are completely normal in a healthy axolotl. None of them needs a fix or a vet visit:

  • Resting on the substrate for hours at a time.
  • Flicking the gills in rhythmic pulses.
  • Walking slowly across the bottom on all four legs.
  • Drifting across the tank with an undulating, eel-like motion.
  • Gulping air at the surface, because axolotls have lungs.
  • Lying on top of plants or decor after a big meal.

A lot of "weird" axolotl behavior is just this list at work. The skill is knowing when a behavior leaves it.

Why is my axolotl floating at the surface?

Floating is caused by gas in the gut, swallowed air, or water that is too warm. Axolotls have no swim bladder, which means they are naturally negatively buoyant; when one rides high, excess gas is pushing it up. Overfeeding, gulping air during a meal, and a bubbler that makes ingestible bubbles are the usual sources of that gas.

If the floating follows a large meal, it resolves within 24-48 hours. If it persists past two days, test the water and check the temperature first.

What does a stressed axolotl look like?

A stressed axolotl curls its gills sharply forward, curls its tail tip, and either hides constantly or swims frantically. These three signals trace back to the same two causes: poor water quality and high temperature. Test ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate, confirm the tank sits at 16-18°C (60-64°F), and cut disturbance.

What do concerning behaviors mean?

Seven behaviors map to a specific cause and a specific fix. Each one is handled the same way, with a water test and a temperature check:

BehaviorCauseFix
Floating at the surfaceGas, overfeeding, warm waterCheck temperature; fast for 1 day
Gills curled forwardStress, poor water, high temperatureTest water; reduce disturbance
Frantic swimming or dartingStrong filter flow, new tankSlow the filter; add hides
Refusing food for 2+ daysStress, temperature, impactionTest water; check temperature
Flipping or thrashingWater-quality crisisEmergency water change
Scratching against decorSkin irritation, parasitesTest water; treat for parasites
Swimming upside downGut issues, severe stressFast; check temperature

A single floaty day after a big meal is not a problem. Repeated or worsening behaviors require a water test and a temperature check before any other step.

When is an axolotl most active?

An axolotl is most active at night and in low light. It hunts as a crepuscular and nocturnal predator, which means the liveliest swimming happens after the lights go out. Dim lighting and a hiding spot bring out natural activity and keep daytime stress low.

If your axolotl is calm all day and lively at dusk, that schedule is normal.

What happens after feeding?

A well-fed axolotl settles back to the bottom and rests while it digests. It snaps at food quickly, chews briefly, and swallows. After a large meal, a quiet drift near the bottom or a short float is normal digestion, not illness.

If the animal spits food out repeatedly or floats after every meal, review portion size and water temperature, because overfeeding causes both symptoms.

Why does an axolotl hide all day?

Daytime hiding is normal for a nocturnal animal, especially in a new tank. The adjustment period typically lasts a few days to a week, after which the axolotl comes out more.

If hiding pairs with forward-curled gills or a refusal to eat for more than two days, treat it as a stress signal and test the water.

Is it normal for an axolotl to stay still for hours?

Yes, long stillness is normal resting behavior. Axolotls spend much of the day motionless on the bottom, so hours without movement are not a concern by themselves.

Stillness becomes a problem only when it pairs with forward-curled gills, a bent tail tip, or a loss of appetite.

Why is my axolotl swimming frantically?

Frantic swimming is caused by strong filter flow or the stress of a new environment. Slow the filter, add hiding spots, and give the animal several days to settle.

Why are my axolotl's gills curled forward?

Forward-curled gills are a stress signal with three common causes: poor water quality, high temperature, and disturbance. Test the water and confirm the tank is in the 16-18°C (60-64°F) range.

Do axolotls need companions?

No, axolotls are solitary animals, so a single axolotl lives a complete, healthy life alone.

When do I worry about a behavior change?

Worry when the behavior is repeated, pairs with forward-curled gills or a bent tail tip, or lasts more than 72 hours. A single odd episode, such as a floaty day after a big meal, is not a problem.

Behavior makes sense once you measure it against the normal baseline. Test the water and check the temperature first, because those two numbers explain most behavior changes.
