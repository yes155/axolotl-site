What is an axolotl?

An axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum) is a fully aquatic salamander native to Mexico, famous for keeping its larval features for life and regenerating lost body parts. It is an amphibian, not a fish, and it lives entirely in fresh water.

Why are axolotls famous?

Axolotls are famous for regeneration: they regrow limbs, tail, gills, jaw, spinal cord, heart tissue, and parts of the brain, without scarring, for their whole lives. That ability makes them the vertebrate champions of regeneration and one of the most studied animals in biology.

Regeneration works through blastema cells, which revert the wound tissue to a less-specialized state and rebuild the missing structure. Because they do not scar, researchers study them for human tissue-repair applications.

What is neoteny?

Neoteny is the retention of juvenile features into adulthood, and axolotls are its classic example because they keep their feathery external gills, finned tail, and aquatic lifestyle for life. Most amphibians metamorphose into land forms, but the axolotl stays in its larval form unless artificially induced to change.

That is why pet axolotls always look young. They never leave the water, and a fully grown 12-inch axolotl still breathes through gills like a larva.

Where do axolotls live?

Wild axolotls live in one place: the Xochimilco lake and canal system within Mexico City. Historically they also lived in Lake Chalco, which has since been drained, so Xochimilco is now the species' only wild home.

Today almost every axolotl on Earth lives in captivity. Laboratories, zoos, and home aquariums hold the vast majority of the species, because the wild population is critically endangered.

How many axolotls are left in the wild?

Only 50-1,000 wild axolotls remain, according to the IUCN Red List, which classifies the species as critically endangered. Pollution, urbanization, and invasive fish like tilapia and perch have driven the collapse.

Captive numbers are far higher and stable. The pet trade and research labs rely entirely on captive-bred lines, so captive axolotls are not endangered even though the wild population is.

Are axolotls endangered?

Yes, the wild axolotl is critically endangered on the IUCN Red List, with 50-1,000 individuals left in Xochimilco. The listing reflects ongoing habitat loss and invasive species pressure in its native range.

Captive axolotls carry the species' public image. Every pet and lab axolotl descends from captive-bred stock, so conservation and the hobby run on the same captive population.

How long do axolotls live?

Captive axolotls live 10-15 years with good care, which makes them a long-term pet commitment. Water temperature and quality drive lifespan, and axolotls also retain fertility through their average lifespan, so older individuals still breed.

Cool, clean water is the longevity factor. Keepers who hold the tank at 60-64 F (16-18 C) report animals reaching 12-15 years.

What do axolotls eat?

Axolotls are carnivores that eat worms, insects, crustaceans, mollusks, and small fish, catching prey by suction. In captivity they eat earthworms, blackworms, bloodworms, brine shrimp, and sinking pellets.

They have small, undeveloped teeth, so they swallow prey whole. A female can lay hundreds of eggs per breeding, with external fertilization in the water.

What are 10 amazing biology facts about axolotls?

Here are 10 verified biology facts about the axolotl:

  • Regenerates limbs, tail, gills, jaw, spinal cord, heart tissue, and brain parts, repeatedly and without scarring.
  • Keeps larval features for life through neoteny.
  • Breathes through gills, lungs, and skin.
  • Spans about 32 billion base pairs of genome, roughly 10 times the human genome.
  • Grows indeterminately, gaining size throughout life.
  • Relies on a largely cartilaginous skeleton, which makes the body delicate.
  • Reproduces by external fertilization, with females laying hundreds of eggs.
  • Retains fertility throughout its average lifespan.
  • Uses a lateral line of sensory cells to detect water movement.
  • Serves as a model organism whose genome is fully sequenced.

What are 10 fun axolotl facts?

Here are 10 fun facts about the axolotl:

  • Derives its name from Nahuatl, translating to water monster or water dog.
  • Appears in Aztec mythology as the god Xolotl transformed to escape sacrifice.
  • Appears on the Mexican 50-peso banknote.
  • Earns the nickname Mexican walking fish, despite being an amphibian.
  • Always appears to smile, because the fixed mouth curves upward.
  • Stays aquatic for life, one of few vertebrates to do so.
  • Hunts at night using smell and lateral-line sensors.
  • Shows dark wild-type color, while pink and white morphs are captive mutants.
  • Metamorphoses only when induced with thyroid hormone, which shortens lifespan.
  • Has been bred in laboratories for over a century.

What are 5 facts about keeping pet axolotls?

Here are 5 pet-keeping facts about axolotls:

  • Needs a minimum 20-gallon long tank for one adult.
  • Needs cool water at 60-64 F (16-18 C), with no heater.
  • Lives alone, because fish nibble gills.
  • Stays out of hands, because the skin is delicate and permeable.
  • Comes in leucistic, golden albino, melanoid, and wild-type morphs.

Are axolotls really walking fish?

No, axolotls are not fish; they are aquatic salamanders, and the nickname dates to their four legs and water-walking gait. They have four limbs and external gills, and they walk along the tank bottom, which is how the name stuck.

The name is a historical nickname, not biology. Axolotls belong to the order Caudata, which contains all salamanders.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do axolotls turn into salamanders?

Most do not, because axolotls are already salamanders that remain in their larval form for life through neoteny. Induced metamorphosis is possible but stressful and shortens lifespan.

Can an axolotl regrow its brain?

Yes, axolotls regrow parts of their brain and heart, in addition to limbs, tail, gills, and jaw. This regeneration is lifelong and scar-free.

Why is the axolotl called a walking fish?

It is a historical nickname based on its four legs and aquatic walking, even though it is an amphibian, not a fish. The name comes from the Nahuatl word axolotl.

How big do axolotls get?

Adult axolotls reach 9-12 inches (23-30 cm) from snout to tail tip, usually by 18-24 months. Growth continues slowly through life.
