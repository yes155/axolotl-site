Axolotl Care Guide: Tank, Water, Diet & Health

Axolotl care is built around a cool, stable aquatic environment, an established biological filter, safe housing, appropriate food, regular water testing, and early recognition of health changes. This guide gives the whole system in one place and routes each specialist task to its canonical page.

Good axolotl care is mostly environmental management. Keep the animal in a cool, stable freshwater aquarium with established biological filtration, gentle circulation, safe surfaces, appropriate food and regular water testing. Avoid trying to diagnose or treat a health problem from one visible sign; measure the environment first and use the specialist health page for the specific symptom.

This is the overview page. It owns the broad “how do I care for an axolotl?” question. Tank size, temperature, water chemistry, feeding schedules, specific diseases, legality and buying decisions are owned by the specialist guides linked below.

What is an axolotl?

Ambystoma mexicanum is a Mexican salamander that normally becomes reproductively mature while retaining an aquatic body plan, including external gills. This developmental strategy is commonly described as paedomorphosis or neoteny.

For the entity definition and core facts, use What Is an Axolotl?. For fish-versus-amphibian classification, use Is an Axolotl an Amphibian?.

Is an axolotl the right pet for you?

An axolotl can suit someone who is comfortable maintaining an aquarium and prefers an animal that is watched rather than handled. The main commitment is not daily interaction; it is keeping the environment consistently appropriate over many years.

Before buying, make sure you can:

  • maintain cool water in your actual room through the hottest part of the year;
  • test water chemistry and understand the nitrogen cycle;
  • provide an appropriately sized aquarium and stand;
  • source suitable food consistently;
  • arrange exotic/amphibian veterinary care if needed; and
  • verify that ownership and transport are legal where you live.

Use Are Axolotls Good Pets? for the pros, cons and beginner-difficulty decision.

What size tank does an adult axolotl need?

Use the dedicated tank-size page as the source of truth. Current Petco care guidance recommends a minimum of 20+ gallons for one adult and says length matters more than height. MyAxolotl treats a 20-gallon long as the practical lower bound and prefers a 40-gallon breeder as a more forgiving permanent home when space and budget permit.

The larger recommendation is a practical site preference, not a claim that one exact gallon number is a universal biological threshold. Animal number, floor area, filtration and husbandry intensity all matter.

See What Size Tank Does an Axolotl Need?.

How cool should axolotl water be?

Cool and stable matters more than chasing one magic number. The Ambystoma Genetic Stock Center maintains its research colony in cool water, and current amphibian veterinary guidance emphasizes species-appropriate stable temperature because temperature affects metabolism and immune function.

For a home aquarium, measure the water continuously or at least daily during warm periods and build a cooling plan around the warmest room conditions you actually experience. A fan, room air conditioning or an aquarium chiller may be appropriate depending on the heat load.

Use Axolotl Tank Temperature for the current home target and Aquarium Chillers if active cooling is needed.

What water quality does an axolotl need?

Amphibians absorb water and dissolved substances readily across their skin, so clean water is foundational. Merck's 2026 amphibian guidance advises regular testing of aquatic systems for temperature, pH, hardness, ammonia, nitrite and nitrate, and emphasizes water free from chlorine, ammonia and nitrite.

Do not use a broad care page to memorize a long table of fixed thresholds. Test the tank, understand the nitrogen cycle and use the specialist page to interpret the actual readings.

Start with How to Test Axolotl Water and then use Water Parameters & Nitrogen Cycle.

Does an axolotl need a filter?

For a conventional home aquarium, established biological filtration is the normal approach. The goal is enough biological capacity to process nitrogenous waste without creating a strong current across the animal's resting area.

Do not size the filter from an invented axolotl turnover multiplier. Filter choice depends on biological-media capacity, actual waste load, mechanical debris capture and whether the output can be made gentle. See Do Axolotls Need a Filter? and Canister vs Sponge.

How often should you change the water?

Regular partial water replacement is still necessary in a filtered aquarium. The 2024 AGSC guide and Merck's 2026 pet-amphibian guidance both support routine weekly partial renewal in established aquatic systems, while the amount needed can change with measured chemistry and waste load.

Use How to Change Axolotl Tank Water rather than following an automatic percentage from an overview article.

What substrate is safe?

Avoid loose gravel or objects small enough to be swallowed. Axolotls use suction feeding and can ingest material around the food. Substrate choice should account for animal size, grain size, cleanliness and the keeper's ability to remove waste.

The Substrate Guide owns the safe-choice comparison; Gravel Risks explains the foreign-body hazard.

What hides, plants and lighting are appropriate?

Provide smooth hiding places and avoid sharp décor or openings that can trap the animal. Axolotls do not need bright display lighting for their own benefit, so any light used for viewing or plants should still leave shaded/refuge areas available.

For plants and décor, use Live vs Artificial Plants and the Tank Setup Guide.

What do axolotls eat?

Axolotls are carnivorous. Captive diets commonly use appropriately sized earthworms/nightcrawlers and nutritionally suitable sinking pellets, while larvae require much smaller live foods during early development. Feeding frequency changes with life stage, body condition, food size and the individual.

Use What Do Axolotls Eat? for food choice and Feeding Schedule by Age for routine frequency.

Can axolotls live with other animals?

Do not add fish or another axolotl simply because the tank has enough gallons. Tank mates can introduce nipping, predation, food competition, disease and ingestion hazards. Multiple axolotls also need close size matching and monitoring.

Use Axolotl Tank Mates for other species and Can Axolotls Live Together? for same-species cohabitation.

Can you hold or pet an axolotl?

Routine petting is unnecessary. Amphibian skin is biologically important and easily disturbed, so handle only when the animal must be transferred for safety, veterinary care or essential husbandry.

Use Can You Hold an Axolotl? for transfer methods and Are Axolotls Poisonous or Dangerous? for human-hygiene and bite questions.

How do you know when an axolotl is unwell?

Look for a change from the animal's normal pattern, not one internet “diagnostic sign.” Concerning changes can involve appetite, body condition, gills, skin, wounds, buoyancy, swimming, breathing, fecal production or activity. The same sign can have more than one cause.

When something changes:

  1. measure temperature and water chemistry;
  2. record appetite, feces, posture, behavior and recent changes;
  3. take clear dated photographs if there is a visible lesion;
  4. follow the specialist page for the specific sign; and
  5. seek an amphibian-experienced veterinarian for severe, worsening or persistent problems.

Use Axolotl Stress Signs as the symptom router and Axolotl Emergency Signs for urgent triage.

Before/after changesRecord new equipment, food, animals, treatments or major maintenance so later health changes have context.

How much does axolotl ownership cost?

The animal's purchase price is only one part of ownership. Aquarium, stand, filtration, testing supplies, cooling, food, electricity and veterinary care can exceed the cost of the axolotl itself. Use Monthly Cost of Ownership and Axolotl Price by Morph.

Is it legal to own an axolotl?

Rules can change by country, state/province and locality. Do not rely on an old care article or a seller's willingness to ship. Check the current primary-source rules for your jurisdiction before buying or transporting an animal.

Use the Axolotl Legality Guide.
