Axolotl Anatomy: Gills and Lungs

Do axolotls have lungs?

Yes, axolotls have lungs, plus external gills and oxygen-absorbing skin. They breathe through all three systems, with the feathery external gills and the skin doing most of the work underwater. The lungs are simple sacs used for surface gulps, which makes the axolotl one of the most flexible breathers among amphibians.

A true amphibian built for a life spent entirely underwater keeps this gill-lung-skin combo its whole life.

How do axolotls breathe?

Axolotls breathe through three systems at once: external gills, skin, and lungs. Each system has a specific job:

SystemWhat it does
External gillsThree feathery gills per side extract oxygen from water
SkinAbsorbs oxygen directly through the moist skin
LungsSimple sacs gulp air at the surface
Gill flickingMoves water over the gill filaments to boost oxygen uptake
RegenerationGills regrow completely if damaged or nipped

The gills and skin handle the routine oxygen supply, and the lungs top up the oxygen when the animal surfaces.

How many gills does an axolotl have?

An axolotl has six external gills, three on each side of the head. Each gill stalk carries fine filaments called fimbriae that increase the surface area for absorbing oxygen. The large, feathery build exists because the gills carry the main oxygen load for a fully aquatic animal.

Why are axolotl gills so large?

The gills are large because they are the primary oxygen organs for an animal that never leaves the water. Each of the three stalks per side is lined with fimbriae that maximize gas exchange. The gills also work as a health indicator, because they change shape with stress and water quality.

Why does my axolotl go to the surface for air?

surface gulping tops up oxygen and is normal behavior. The lungs make the gulps possible, so an occasional trip to the surface is healthy, not a warning sign. Frequent gulping means the water holds too little oxygen, which happens when the water is too warm, the filter is weak, or the tank is overstocked.

If gulping becomes constant, cool the tank and service the filter before anything else.

Can axolotls breathe out of water?

No, axolotls survive out of water for only seconds before damage starts. The gills collapse and dry out in air, which means the animal is fully aquatic for life. It can gulp a breath at the surface, but it cannot live outside the water.

What do healthy axolotl gills look like?

Healthy gills are full, fluffy, and curve backward from the head. Thin, pale, forward-curled, or shrinking gills indicate stress or poor water conditions. The gills recover when the cause is fixed, which makes them the most readable health gauge an owner has.

What is buccal pumping?

Buccal pumping is the throat movement that draws water over the gills, an axolotl's version of a breath. The throat membrane pulses to pull oxygenated water across the gill filaments, which is why the gills keep moving even at rest. Combined with the three-chambered heart, the system moves oxygen without relying on lungs the way mammals do.

Do axolotls have lungs or gills?

Axolotls have both lungs and gills, plus skin respiration. The gills and skin dominate underwater, and the lungs provide surface gulps. All three systems together keep the animal alive across a range of water conditions.

What happens to axolotl gills in poor water?

In poor water, the gills curl forward, shrink, and lose their feathery fringe. Ammonia, nitrite, and nitrate damage the gill tissue directly. Test the water and keep the tank at 16-18°C (60-64°F), and the gills recover their full shape.

The gill-and-lung system makes the axolotl a flexible breather, and its gills make it easy to read. Clean, cool water keeps all three systems working.
