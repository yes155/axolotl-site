Is an axolotl an amphibian?

Yes. The axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum) is an amphibian, specifically a salamander in the order Caudata. It spends its entire life underwater and answers to the nickname Mexican walking fish, but it is not a fish. The axolotl is an amphibian that simply never completes the usual amphibian transformation to land, which is exactly why it keeps looking like an oversized larva.

Which class do axolotls belong to?

Axolotls belong to the class Amphibia, the amphibians, grouped with frogs, toads, newts, and other salamanders. They are vertebrates with backbones, and within Amphibia they sit in the order Caudata. Amphibians are cold-blooded animals that lay eggs in water and start life as aquatic larvae, and the axolotl follows that pattern except for the final metamorphosis.

Are axolotls fish?

No. An axolotl is an amphibian, not a fish. Fish have internal gills, scales, and fin-based skeletons; axolotls have feathery external gills, moist skin, and four legs. The Mexican walking fish name is a misnomer that stuck because the axolotl is aquatic and gilled, so the differences from a fish are easy to confuse at a glance.

Fish keep internal gills and bony skeletons built for water, while reptiles have scaly, dry skin and breathe only with lungs. The axolotl's moist skin, external gills, and amphibian skeleton settle the matter.

Are axolotls reptiles?

No. Axolotls are amphibians, so they are never reptiles. Reptiles are cold-blooded but wear scaly, dry skin and breathe with lungs; axolotls have permeable skin, external gills, and no scales. The cold-blooded trait is the only one they share, which is one difference out of four.

Why does an axolotl stay in the water?

The axolotl stays in the water because it is neotenic: it keeps its larval features for life instead of metamorphosing. Its thyroid system never triggers the full transformation that turns a salamander larva into a land adult, so it keeps gills, a finned tail, and an aquatic lifestyle. It is not a juvenile stuck in time; it is a mature adult that reproduces in its larval body.

What does amphibian biology mean for axolotl care?

Amphibian biology sets five concrete care rules for axolotls.

  • Amphibian skin absorbs chemicals, so hands are washed free of soap and lotion before any handling.
  • Moist, permeable skin means the animal lives fully aquatic at all times.
  • Eggs are laid in water during cool-season breeding.
  • Cold-blooded metabolism means water temperature drives appetite and activity, so water stays at 60-64 F (16-18 C).
  • The skin does much of the gas exchange, which makes ammonia and nitrite poisoning fast-acting.

Because the axolotl is an amphibian rather than a fish, its care centers on water quality and temperature, not on feeding schedules.

Is an axolotl a salamander?

Yes. Axolotls are salamanders in the order Caudata, family Ambystomatidae. They are the best-known neotenic salamanders.

Why is my axolotl always in the water?

Because it is neotenic. The axolotl keeps its larval aquatic form for life and never becomes a land-dwelling adult, so it lives underwater permanently.

The axolotl is the amphibian that rewrote the amphibian rulebook: a salamander that chose to stay a larva forever.
