What is neoteny?

Neoteny is the retention of juvenile or larval features into adulthood. The axolotl (Ambystoma mexicanum) is the textbook case: it grows to full adult size, becomes sexually mature at 18-27 months, and still keeps the feathery gills, finned tail, and aquatic lifestyle of a salamander larva. Neoteny is not a defect; it is a successful evolutionary strategy.

How is neoteny different from metamorphosis?

Metamorphosis transforms a larva into an adult form, and neoteny skips that transformation. A normal salamander larva loses its gills, grows eyelids and stronger lungs, and moves onto land. A neotenic axolotl stays in the water, keeps its gills, and reaches sexual maturity in its larval body.

The table below compares axolotls against salamanders, newts, and frogs that do metamorphose.

Axolotl (A. mexicanum)No, stays larvalAquatic, gilled
Tiger salamander (A. tigrinum)YesTerrestrial adult

The deciding factor is thyroid function: in the axolotl, the pathway that triggers metamorphosis in other salamanders never activates, so the larval program stays on.

What causes neoteny in axolotls?

Axolotls stay in larval form because their thyroid system never triggers metamorphosis. The pituitary produces too little thyroid-stimulating hormone, so the thyroid never releases the thyroxine that transforms a larva into a land adult in other species. The animal is fully grown and fertile; it just never switches its development over.

Is neoteny an advantage?

Yes. In the axolotl's environment, neoteny is a survival advantage. Xochimilco's stable, food-rich water lets axolotls reproduce without the risky journey to land that other salamanders take. Neoteny is not unique to axolotls, since some tiger salamander populations are also neotenic, but the axolotl is the most complete example.

Can an axolotl be forced to metamorphose?

Yes, in a laboratory, but the process shortens lifespan and stresses the animal. Treating an axolotl with thyroid hormone, thyroxine, produces a terrestrial adult that resembles a tiger salamander. Pet axolotls are kept neotenic, because the forced change strips away the biology that makes the species special.

What is neoteny in simple terms?

It is an animal keeping its juvenile or larval features into adulthood instead of transforming into an adult form.

Do axolotls reach full adult size?

Yes. A sexually mature axolotl reaches 15-45 cm (6-18 in), with about 23 cm (9 in) most common.

Why do axolotls stay babies?

Their thyroid system never triggers metamorphosis, so they keep larval features like external gills for their whole lives.

Is an axolotl a baby salamander?

No. It is a mature, reproducing adult that looks like a larva, which is exactly what neoteny means.

Do axolotls ever metamorphose?

Almost never on their own. Thyroid hormone treatment can force it, but it is stressful and shortens lifespan, so it is not done to pets.

Neoteny makes the axolotl one of biology's great oddities: a fully grown animal that never leaves its childhood.
