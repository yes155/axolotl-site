How to Choose a Reputable Axolotl Breeder

How do I choose a reputable axolotl breeder?

Choose a breeder who keeps animals in cycled, cool tanks at 60-64 F, quotes water readings of zero ammonia and zero nitrite, and answers husbandry questions openly. A reputable breeder shows the parents, offers a health guarantee, and provides after-sale support. The breeder choice matters more than the morph choice, because a good breeder produces a healthy 10-15 year pet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Ask these 12 questions before committing to any breeder.

  1. Where were the parents bred, and can I see them?
  1. How long have you been breeding axolotls?
  1. What do you feed your juveniles?
  1. What are your water parameters: temperature, ammonia, and nitrite?
  1. Do you quarantine new stock before adding it?
  1. What morphs and genetics do you produce?
  1. Do you offer a health guarantee?
  1. What happens if the axolotl arrives sick?
  1. Can you show a photo or video of this exact animal?
  1. Do you provide care guidance after the sale?
  1. How do you ship, and what is your live-arrival policy?
  1. Are your animals 100% captive-bred?

A breeder who answers all 12 openly is worth the price, and a breeder who dodges them is not.

What red flags disqualify a breeder?

Six red flags disqualify a breeder, and the first two are deal-breakers.

  • Gravel or pebbles in the tank, which causes impaction that kills.
  • Juveniles under 3 inches (7.6 cm) or under 3 months old, which are too young to ship safely.
  • No photos or videos of the actual animal for sale.
  • No lineage or parent information.
  • Warm water or vagueness about temperature.
  • Payment demanded upfront via gift cards.

The table below pairs each red flag with the safer alternative.

What does a good breeder's setup look like?

A good breeder's tanks are cool, cycled, and uncrowded, with each axolotl in at least 20 gallons of water. The water runs at 60-64 F (16-18 C), juveniles are grouped by size, and males and females are separated. A breeder who houses animals this way knows the species, and one who packs them into warm tubs does not.

Why does genetics matter?

Genetics matter because sibling breeding produces weak, deformed animals that die young. Responsible breeders keep unrelated bloodlines and can describe the parents' backgrounds. A seller who bred sibling to sibling to hit a morph is a serious risk to your axolotl's long-term health.

How do I verify a breeder's reputation?

Ask the axolotl community, because a breeder's trail of posts and buyers is the best signal. Search forums such as Caudata.org, r/axolotls, and breeder directories for the name. Look for repeat buyers, positive care stories, and a seller who posts about their animals; consistent complaints or no online presence are red flags.

How do I find a reputable axolotl breeder?

Look for a long-standing breeder with verifiable photos, open husbandry answers, and a live-arrival guarantee, found through axolotl forums and breeder directories.

Can I trust a breeder who won't show the parents?

No. Refusing to show parent stock is a major red flag and a common sign of a reseller rather than a breeder.

What water do breeder axolotls live in?

Cycled tanks at 60-64 F (16-18 C) with zero ammonia and zero nitrite.

How big is a juvenile before it ships safely?

At least 3 inches (7.6 cm) and about 3-4 months old.

The weeks you spend finding the right breeder are the cheapest insurance you will buy for a 10-15 year pet.
