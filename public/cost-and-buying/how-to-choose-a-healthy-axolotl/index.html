How to Choose a Healthy Axolotl

Before buying, look for normal body condition, intact skin, balanced movement, recent feeding records, and measured water quality. Appearance can reveal warning signs, but a photo cannot diagnose an axolotl.

By Farrukh Abdullah · Edited by Ananda Abidin · Updated 2026-08-27 · 484 words

Before buying an axolotl, check body condition, skin, gills, movement, recent feeding, and the water it is living in. These observations can identify reasons to pause a purchase, but they cannot diagnose a disease from a photo or short visit.

What Should You Check Before Buying?

CheckReassuring observationReason to pause and ask more
Body conditionProportionate body and tail with no marked wasting or sudden swellingVery thin body, pronounced asymmetry, or unexplained swelling
SkinIntact surface appropriate for the morphOpen wounds, bleeding, ulcers, heavy shedding, or attached growth
GillsGill tissue present with no obvious damage or attached materialRapid change, injury, unusual discharge, or severe deterioration
Posture and movementBalanced position and coordinated movement when the animal moves on its ownPersistent inability to stay upright, uncontrolled floating, or poor coordination
Feeding historySeller can name the food, portion, and last accepted mealNo recent record, repeated refusal, or sudden change without explanation
Water recordsSeller provides measured temperature, ammonia, and nitriteOnly says the water is “fine” or cannot provide readings

Can Gills or Skin Diagnose an Illness?

No single visible feature diagnoses an axolotl illness. Gill size and posture vary with anatomy, activity, flow, and environment. White material can have more than one cause. Redness, lesions, lethargy, appetite change, and abnormal position are clinical signs that require context and sometimes veterinary testing.

The Merck Veterinary Manual notes that amphibian infections can produce overlapping signs and may require microscopy, culture, histology, or other diagnostics. Treat a concerning sign as a reason not to complete the purchase until the seller explains it or an exotics-experienced veterinarian assesses it.

Should You Tap the Glass or Demand a Feeding Demonstration?

Do not tap the glass to test an axolotl. Observe undisturbed movement and breathing, and ask the seller for a recent feeding video or written feeding history. An axolotl may not eat on demand during a brief visit, so one refused meal does not prove illness.

A repeated appetite change combined with weight loss, abnormal posture, skin damage, or poor water records is more meaningful than one moment of behavior.

What Records Should You Request?

  • A current photo or video of the exact axolotl
  • Hatch date or age estimate and current length
  • Food type, feeding frequency, and last accepted meal
  • Current temperature, ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, and pH
  • Recent injury, treatment, appetite, buoyancy, or skin history
  • Written pickup, shipping, and live-arrival terms

What Should You Do After Choosing an Axolotl?

Prepare a fully cycled, temperature-appropriate setup before pickup or delivery. If other axolotls are already present, use a separate observation or quarantine setup and discuss an appropriate plan with an exotics-experienced veterinarian. Use the new-axolotl acclimation guide for the handoff.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a healthy axolotl look like before purchase?

Look for proportionate body condition, intact skin, balanced posture, coordinated movement, a documented feeding history, and measured water quality. These observations reduce uncertainty but do not diagnose health.

Do small or curled gills prove an axolotl is sick?

No. Gill appearance can change with anatomy, activity, flow, injury, and environment. A marked or recent change deserves questions, but one gill feature cannot diagnose a disease.

Should the seller feed the axolotl in front of me?

A recent feeding video or written feeding record is useful, but one refused meal during a short visit does not prove illness. Repeated appetite change combined with other warning signs matters more.
