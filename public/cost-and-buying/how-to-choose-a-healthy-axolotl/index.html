How to Choose a Healthy Axolotl

How do I check an axolotl before buying it?

Run an 8-point checklist: gills, skin, tail and limbs, belly, behavior, breathing, appetite, and water quality. A sick axolotl is often fatal before you get it home, so checking these signs takes two minutes and saves months of stress.

The single most reliable signal is the gills. Everything else on the list confirms what the gills already tell you, so start there and work down.

CheckWhat to look for
GillsFull, fluffy, not curled forward; no white fuzz
SkinSmooth, no red patches, no pale spots
Tail and limbsComplete, no missing toes, no open sores
BellyRounded but not bloated; no red streaks
BehaviorActive, not floating limp; responds when tapped gently
BreathingGills flicking regularly; no gasping at surface
AppetiteHas been eating normally, per the seller

What do healthy axolotl gills look like?

Healthy axolotl gills are long, fluffy, and constantly flicking, with no white fuzz and no forward curl. Curled-forward gills signal stress, shortened or shrinking gills point to poor water quality, and white fuzz on the gills is a fungal infection.

Ask the seller to leave the lights on and the tank still for a minute, because gill posture only shows on a calm, unstressed animal.

How do I check an axolotl's skin and belly?

Healthy skin is smooth and even-colored for the morph, and the belly is gently rounded, not bloated or sunken. Red patches or streaks, especially on the belly or legs, can indicate red leg syndrome, a bacterial infection, and pale spots or cottony growths are fungus.

A bloated belly with red streaks is a medical emergency signal. If either shows, skip that animal and look for the next one.

How should a healthy axolotl move?

A healthy axolotl sits on the bottom, flicks its gills, and walks around the tank normally, and it responds to a gentle tap on the glass. Floating at the surface, floating sideways, or sitting limp with curled gills are warning signs.

Movement is the behavior check that confirms everything else. An animal that swims away from a tap but settles back on the bottom is behaving normally.

Why should I ask to see an axolotl eat?

Because a healthy axolotl actively takes food, so an animal that refuses to eat in front of you is the strongest reason to walk away. Never buy an axolotl you have not watched eat.

If the seller is reluctant to feed the animal in front of you, that reluctance alone is a red flag and a reason to end the deal.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a healthy axolotl look like?

Full fluffy gills, smooth skin, an active response, a rounded belly, and a willingness to eat, with no white fuzz, red patches, or floating. Every check on the 8-point list confirms the same picture.

Should I buy an axolotl with small gills?

No, short gills usually mean the animal has been kept in poor water conditions and is stressed or sick, so be cautious and check everything else carefully. Small gills regrow in clean water, but the underlying condition explains why they shrank.

This page is a buyer's checklist and not a veterinary diagnosis. Have a new axolotl checked by an exotic vet within its first week home.
