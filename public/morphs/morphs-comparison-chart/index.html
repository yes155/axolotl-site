Axolotl Morphs Comparison Chart

Nine morphs make up the standard pet-trade lineup: wild type, leucistic, melanoid, golden albino, copper, GFP, piebald, mosaic, and chimera. The four core morphs - wild type, leucistic, melanoid, and golden albino - cover most pet axolotls. The chart below compares all nine by color, rarity, and price.

Which morphs are the most common?

Wild type, leucistic, melanoid, and golden albino are the four common core morphs. These four cover most pet axolotls and sell for $25-$90. A wild type is dark brown or gray with a greenish tint and black speckles, and it always has a gold ring around a black pupil. A leucistic is white with pink gills and black eyes, and a golden albino is gold or yellow with pink or red eyes.

What are all the axolotl morphs?

The nine standard axolotl morphs are wild type, leucistic, melanoid, golden albino, copper, GFP, piebald, mosaic, and chimera. The table below lists each one with its color, rarity, and price:

What are the rarest axolotl morphs?

Chimera and mosaic axolotls are the rarest morphs, because both arise from chance developmental events rather than predictable breeding. A chimera forms when two early embryos fuse into one animal, which produces a clean two-tone split. A mosaic carries two genetically different cell populations, which produces scattered patches. Neither pattern is heritable, so neither is bred to order.

How do I identify my axolotl's morph?

Check the body color, then the eye color, then the animal under a blue light. The eye patterns split the common morphs cleanly: a wild type has a gold ring around a black pupil, a leucistic has solid black eyes, a golden albino has red or pink eyes, and a melanoid has dark eyes with no golden ring. A GFP axolotl glows bright green under blue or black light, and a copper shows a red reflective eye sheen. When the pattern stays ambiguous, ask the breeder for the animal's genetics.

Which morph is best for a beginner?

A wild type or leucistic is the best first axolotl, because both are common, affordable, and hardy. Both sell for $25-$60 and handle standard tank conditions. A melanoid or golden albino works equally well. Rare morphs suit experienced owners, because a $100-$1,000+ animal carries higher price and higher stakes.
