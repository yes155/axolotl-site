Melanoid Axolotl: The Dark Morph

What Is a Melanoid Axolotl?

A melanoid axolotl is a recessive m/m morph that produces extra melanophores and no iridophores, giving it a uniform dark gray or black body with no metallic shine anywhere. It is the darkest standard axolotl morph.

The surplus melanophores make the animal darker than any other regular morph, and the missing iridophores remove every reflective speckle from the skin, gills, fins, and eye ring.

A melanoid axolotl typically costs $40 to $80, depending on how dark and large the animal is.

How Do You Identify a Melanoid Axolotl?

The reliable identification test is the eye: a melanoid shows no reflective ring around the pupil, and a dark wild type shows a metallic ring. The m/m genotype removes iridophores from the eyes as well as the body.

Shine a flashlight at the eye in a dark room. A wild type or leucistic returns a colored metallic flash from the gilded iris, and a melanoid returns a flat black or dark reflection.

A melanoid also reads as flat black or dark gray with no brown undertone and no yellow speckles, while a dark wild type keeps a faint olive-brown cast and some iridescence on the back.

Melanoid Axolotl Genetics

The melanoid morph is caused by two copies of the recessive m allele at the melanoid locus, written m/m. A single M allele produces a normal wild type appearance, so carriers look identical to wild types.

The m allele increases melanophore numbers and blocks iridophore development. Melanoid genes combine freely with the albino (a) and white (d) genes, producing melanoid albinos and melanoid leucistics.

Melanoid vs. Wild Type: Which Is Darker?

A melanoid is always darker than a wild type because it has more melanophores and zero iridophores, while a wild type keeps yellow speckles and a metallic sheen.

A very dark wild type looks black at a glance, but it still shows the reflective eye ring and faint olive or iridescent tones under bright light. A melanoid shows none of these.

If you own a dark axolotl and are not sure which it is, the wild type guide explains the eye test in detail and lists the differences side by side.

Do Melanoid Axolotls Need Different Care?

A melanoid axolotl needs the same care as every other morph, because the mutation changes only skin and eye pigment, never health or temperament.

Melanoids are harder to inspect visually than a leucistic, so gill curl, wounds, and fungus are checked by watching behavior and using a bright light rather than glancing at skin color. Keep the water at 16 to 18 C (60 to 65 F) and start a juvenile on a 20-gallon setup.

The darker color makes melanoids popular for small hides and planted displays. See all axolotl morphs to compare melanoid against leucistic and wild type before you choose.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a melanoid axolotl cost?

A melanoid axolotl costs $40 to $80, and extra-dark individuals sell for more.

Is a black axolotl the same as a melanoid?

The only truly black axolotl is a melanoid; dark wild types keep a brown-olive cast and a shiny eye ring.

Why does my melanoid not have a shiny ring around its eye?

The m/m genotype removes iridophores from the entire body, including the eye ring, which is exactly how a melanoid is identified.

Can a melanoid breed true?

Two melanoids (m/m x m/m) produce 100% melanoid offspring, so the morph breeds true when both parents are melanoids.
