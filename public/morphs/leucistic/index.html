Leucistic Axolotl: The "Lucy" Morph

What Is a Leucistic Axolotl?

A leucistic axolotl is a pale pink to white axolotl with dark eyes and bright pink or red gills, produced by the recessive d/d genotype at the white locus. It is the most common morph in the pet trade after the wild type.

The d/d white-locus pair blocks pigment cells from migrating across the body, so most of the skin stays unpigmented. The eyes keep their dark detail because melanophores concentrate in the head and eye tissue during development.

A leucistic axolotl typically costs $35 to $70, depending on age and gill quality.

How Is a Leucistic Different from an Albino?

A leucistic axolotl has dark eyes, and an albino axolotl has pink or red eyes. The two morphs fail at different stages of pigment production and look similar only at a glance.

An albino (a/a) lacks the enzyme tyrosinase, so it produces no dark melanin anywhere, including the eyes. A leucistic produces melanin normally, but the d/d genotype stops the pigment cells from spreading over the body.

The eye test is conclusive: dark eyes identify a leucistic, and pink or red eyes identify an albino.

What Is a Dirty Lucy Axolotl?

A dirty lucy is a leucistic axolotl that grows dark freckles, spots, or patches of pigment on its white body as it matures. The extra pigment appears when melanophores settle into localized regions instead of staying confined to the head.

The pattern varies between individuals. Some dirty lucies show only a few spots on the back, while others develop dense pigment patches that cover large parts of the body and tail.

A dirty lucy carries the same d/d genetics as a plain leucistic, so the freckles do not change how the morph is bred or inherited.

How Do Leucistic Axolotl Crosses Come Out?

Two leucistic parents produce 100% leucistic offspring, and a leucistic crossed with a wild type produces 100% wild-type-looking carriers.

The table below shows the three common crosses and their offspring:

Lucy x LucyLeucistic100%
Lucy x Wild TypeWild type carrier (D/d)100%
Lucy x Carrier (D/d)Lucy + carrier50% each

The full genotype of a leucistic is d/d with functional A, M, and Ax alleles, so any other recessive gene a parent carries surfaces in the next generation only when both parents carry it.

Do Leucistic Axolotls Need Different Care?

A leucistic axolotl needs identical care to every other morph, because color genetics do not affect health, temperament, or husbandry. The tank size, water temperature, filtration, and diet are the same as for a wild type.

Leucistics show stress and illness easily on their pale skin, which makes gill curl, redness, and fungus simpler to notice than on a dark morph. Keep the tank at 16 to 18 C (60 to 65 F) and give an adult a 20-gallon tank with a gentle filter.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a leucistic axolotl cost?

A leucistic axolotl costs $35 to $70, with show-grade gill quality priced higher.

Do leucistic axolotls turn white as they grow?

Leucistics stay pale pink-white for life; the pink tint on the gills and body comes from blood flow and shifts with temperature and stress.

What is the rarest type of leucistic?

Dirty lucies with heavy, even pigment are uncommon and command a premium.

How do I tell a leucistic from a white albino?

Look at the eyes: a leucistic has dark eyes, and a white albino (a/a d/d) has pink-red eyes.

How Rare Is a Leucistic Axolotl, and What Does One Cost?

The leucistic is one of the two most common morphs in the pet trade, so it is neither rare nor expensive. Pets start around $25–$60, rising only for specific lines or high-quality dirty-lucy patterns. Leucistic vs Golden Albino at a Glance

Both morphs look pale pink to white, which is why they are confused. The easy difference is the eyes: leucistic axolotls keep dark eyes, while golden albino axolotls have red or pink eyes because they cannot make melanin at all. Leucistic animals also keep their natural pigment cells everywhere except the skin, which is limited to the gills and the occasional spot. Read the full breakdown on the golden albino page.
