Copper Axolotl

What Is a Copper Axolotl?

A copper axolotl is a brown to coppery-orange morph with reddish gills and pale tan or reddish eyes, caused by a recessive mutation in the Tyrp1 gene on chromosome 6.

The Tyrp1 mutation blocks the normal eumelanin pathway and steers pigment production toward pheomelanin, the reddish-orange pigment that gives the animal its copper color.

A copper axolotl typically costs $100 to $200, because the genetics are rarer and the morph takes more generations to produce.

How Do You Identify a Copper Axolotl?

A copper axolotl shows a red-orange body, red-pink gills, and light eyes, with no dark brown or black anywhere on the skin.

Shine a light at the eye: a copper reflects a reddish or copper glow, unlike the golden flash of a wild type or the flat eye of a melanoid.

Coppers are frequently confused with melanoid albinos and light wild types, but the red-orange pigment on the body and the light eye color together identify a copper.

The copper gene stacks on other colors. A copper albino is white with copper-toned patches, and a copper x leucistic cross produces coppers with dark eyes.

Copper Axolotl Genetics

The copper morph is caused by two copies of the recessive copper allele at the Tyrp1 locus, so a copper x copper cross produces 100% copper offspring.

The table below shows the three common copper crosses:

Copper inheritance is independent of the albino, melanoid, and white genes, so a breeder combines copper with other morphs using standard Punnett-square math.

Breeding Copper Axolotls

To breed copper axolotls, cross two coppers for 100% copper offspring, or cross two carriers and keep the 25% that are copper.

Keep known carriers labeled, because copper carriers look like ordinary wild types. A single copper x wild type cross produces only carriers, never visible coppers.

After the coppers hatch, treat eggs and larvae with the standard food and temperature routine. The egg and larvae care guide covers feeding and grow-out, and the breeding triggers guide explains how to cycle tanks to get a spawn.

Do Copper Axolotls Need Different Care?

A copper axolotl needs the same tank, temperature, water, and diet as any morph, because the Tyrp1 mutation affects only pigment.

Copper water values are standard: 16-18C, pH 7.4-8.0, and an ammonia- and nitrite-free tank. Coppers eat the usual carnivore diet of earthworms, pellets, and frozen food.

Plan the genetics before breeding so you do not accidentally ship wild types as coppers. Start with the morph list and the Punnett-square guide to set up the cross.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a copper axolotl cost?

A copper axolotl costs $100 to $200, and copper variants such as copper albinos sell for more.

Is the copper axolotl a natural mutation?

Yes, copper is a spontaneous recessive mutation in the Tyrp1 gene that breeders selected and established as a line.

What is the difference between copper and melanoid?

Copper produces red-orange pheomelanin and lacks dark eumelanin, while melanoid produces excess dark pigment and no iridophores.

Do copper axolotls breed true?

Yes, two coppers produce 100% copper offspring because the copper allele is recessive and both parents are homozygous.
