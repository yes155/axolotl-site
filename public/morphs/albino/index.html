Albino Axolotl: Color, Genetics, Types & Identification

An albino axolotl is homozygous for a recessive mutation that disrupts melanin production. Because other pigment systems can remain, albino animals are not all the same color: golden, white-albino and axanthic-albino combinations can look different.

Albino axolotls cannot make normal dark melanin because of a recessive mutation affecting the tyrosinase gene. The term albino describes that loss of melanin production; it does not guarantee one exact body color because yellow pigment, reflective cells and other pigment mutations can change the final appearance.

What is an albino axolotl?

In the laboratory axolotl lineage, the classic albino allele was introduced historically through a cross with an albino tiger salamander. Modern genetic work mapped the albino phenotype to tyrosinase (tyr), a key melanin-synthesis gene. Animals with two copies of the recessive albino allele lack normal melanin production.

What causes albinism in axolotls?

Research identified a disruptive change in the axolotl tyr allele associated with the historic albino phenotype. Because tyrosinase is required for melanin synthesis, the melanophores can be present but cannot produce normal dark melanin.

Other pigment systems are not automatically removed. That is why an otherwise wild-type albino can still show strong yellow coloration.

What does an albino axolotl look like?

An albino axolotl commonly has pale reddish or pinkish eyes because dark melanin is missing. Body color depends on the other pigment traits present:

  • Golden albino: yellow/gold body with reddish or pink eyes.
  • White albino: pale white/pink body from combining the white phenotype with albinism.
  • Axanthic albino: very pale animal because both melanin and normal yellow/reflective pigment systems are altered.

This is why “albino = white” is too simple for axolotls.

Golden albino vs albino: what's the difference?

Golden albino is one visible albino combination, not a separate definition of albinism. The Ambystoma Genetic Stock Center describes an otherwise wild-type albino as yellow with reddish eyes and notes that this appearance is often called a golden albino. The gold comes from retained yellow pigments rather than melanin.

For the specific yellow-gold phenotype, see the golden albino axolotl guide.

What is a white albino axolotl?

A white albino combines the white phenotype with the albino mutation. It can resemble an ordinary leucistic/white axolotl at first glance, but the eyes are pale or pinkish rather than dark because melanin production is also absent.

What is an axanthic albino axolotl?

An axanthic albino combines albinism with the axanthic mutation. AGSC strain notes describe young animals of this genotype as nearly colorless, with older animals sometimes becoming pale yellow as dietary riboflavins accumulate.

Are albino axolotls rare?

“Albino” is a genetic phenotype, not a permanent market-rarity category. Albino stocks are well established in captive axolotls and are maintained by the Ambystoma Genetic Stock Center. Local availability and price depend on breeder supply, lineage, additional traits and region rather than on albinism alone.

Use the morph comparison for appearance and the price-by-morph guide for current market context.

Do albino axolotls need different care?

Core husbandry is the same: stable cool water, appropriate filtration, suitable food, safe substrate and hides. Pale animals may be visually more sensitive to bright display lighting, but color does not create a separate temperature, water-chemistry or feeding requirement.
