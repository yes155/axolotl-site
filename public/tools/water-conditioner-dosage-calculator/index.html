🚫
Never use iodine-based products Iodine and Betadine are lethal to axolotls even at very low concentrations. Check all labels carefully.

Conditioner ≠ biological filtration Water conditioners detoxify chlorine and chloramine but do not establish the nitrogen cycle. A cycled filter is essential.

Getting the Dose Right: More Than a Formality

When it comes to axolotl care, few things are as deceptively routine — yet genuinely consequential — as the water conditioner dose. Too little, and your tap water's chlorine or chloramine remains active, burning the delicate, feathery gills that make axolotls so iconic. Too much, and you risk oxygen depletion: some conditioners (including Seachem Prime) work partly by converting toxins into forms that temporarily consume dissolved oxygen. Overdosing repeatedly can stress a healthy axolotl and make a sick one significantly worse.

The correct dose is the amount that neutralizes exactly the volume of new water entering the tank — no more, no less. For a 25% water change on a 40-gallon tank, you're treating 10 gallons, not 40. Calculating based on tank volume wastes product and can impair water chemistry. This calculator does the math automatically so that there's no ambiguity.

Chlorine vs. Chloramine: Why It Matters

Most municipal water supplies no longer use chlorine alone. Instead, they use chloramine — a stable compound formed by bonding chlorine with ammonia. Chloramine is far harder to remove than chlorine: it cannot be eliminated by aeration or letting water sit overnight, and it is not neutralized by basic sodium thiosulfate-based conditioners.

High-quality conditioners like Seachem Prime, Fritz Complete, and Seachem Safe are specifically formulated to break the chloramine bond, neutralizing both the chlorine and the released ammonia. If you're using a basic conditioner that only states "dechlorinator," always verify it also addresses chloramine — this is especially important in urban water systems. Call your local water authority or check their annual water quality report if you're unsure what your tap water contains.

Why Conditioner Doesn't Replace Cycling

Water conditioner is a water preparation tool, not a filtration system. A new or uncycled aquarium has no established colony of beneficial bacteria (primarily Nitrosomonas and Nitrobacter species) to convert ammonia into nitrite and then nitrate. Adding conditioner to an uncycled tank will momentarily detoxify ammonia, but it will not prevent more ammonia from building up from your axolotl's waste within hours.

An axolotl needs a fully cycled filter with established bacterial colonies before it is added to the tank. Conditioner is a tool used at water change time to make tap water safe — not a permanent solution to high ammonia or poor water quality. If your ammonia reads above 0 ppm between water changes in an established tank, this signals an imbalance in your biological filtration, not a conditioner dosing issue.

Emergency Dosing: When to Use 5× Prime

Seachem Prime's most powerful feature — and its greatest risk if misused — is its emergency dosing capability. At up to five times the normal dose, Prime temporarily detoxifies ammonia and nitrite by binding them in a form that is safe to axolotls but still readable by standard test kits (which is why your test kit may continue to show ammonia even after dosing — this is normal). This emergency window lasts approximately 24–48 hours, giving you time to perform a water change or fix the underlying cause.

However, this 5× dose carries real risks in a low-oxygen environment. Prime's detoxification mechanism consumes dissolved oxygen. In a warm tank, a tank with heavy axolotl load, or a tank without adequate surface agitation, emergency dosing can cause oxygen depletion. Always ensure maximum surface movement during and after emergency dosing, and retest water parameters within 24 hours to assess whether the situation has stabilized or worsened.

The Aloe Vera Problem for Axolotls

Many popular fish conditioners — including certain API products and "slime coat" enhancers — contain aloe vera as a coating agent. While this is generally harmless or even beneficial for bony fish, it is dangerous for axolotls. Axolotls have extremely permeable, mucus-coated skin and delicate external gill filaments. Aloe vera creates an artificial coating that can disrupt their natural mucous layer, smother gill filaments, and impair the gas exchange they depend on. Always read conditioner labels in full before using any new product. The safest choices specifically developed or verified for axolotls are Seachem Prime, Seachem Safe, and Seachem's own Axolotl Conditioner formulation.

Why Regular Water Testing Is Non-Negotiable

No calculator can substitute for regular water testing. The correct dose of conditioner is determined partly by what's in your tap water — and municipal water chemistry changes seasonally, especially after heavy rainfall events that alter reservoir chemistry. Using a quality liquid test kit (API Master Test Kit or equivalent) at least once per week gives you an accurate picture of ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, and pH levels. This data, combined with the correct conditioner dose, forms the foundation of a healthy axolotl environment. Your axolotl's gills will show you when something's wrong — pale, frayed, or curling gills are a sign of water quality stress — but by then, you're already reacting to a problem rather than preventing one. Test proactively, dose precisely, and change water consistently.
