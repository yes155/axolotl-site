Axolotl Tank Size by Age

Axolotl tank size depends on the animal's age: a baby needs 5-10 gallons, a juvenile needs 20 gallons, an adult needs 20-40 gallons, and a pair needs 40 gallons or more. Floor space matters more than water volume, because axolotls are bottom-dwelling animals. The table below matches every stage to its minimum tank.

How many gallons does an axolotl need by age?

A baby axolotl needs 5-10 gallons, a juvenile needs 20 gallons, an adult needs 20-40 gallons, and an adult pair needs 40 gallons. The table below lists each stage with its recommended minimum:

Axolotl stageRecommended minimum tank
Baby (under 3 in / 7 cm)5-10 gallon grow-out tub or tank
Juvenile (3-6 in / 7-15 cm)20 gallon long
Adult (over 6 in / 15 cm)20-40 gallon long (30+ ideal)
Adult pair40 gallon breeder or larger

Every tank in the table is a long format, because height adds nothing for a bottom-dwelling animal.

Why does floor space matter more than gallons?

Floor space matters more than gallons because axolotls spend their lives on the bottom, so footprint is the measurement that counts. A 20-gallon long measures 30 x 12 inches of floor, while a 20-gallon tall measures 24 x 12 inches; both hold 20 gallons of water. More water volume also stabilizes chemistry, so a 40-gallon tank holds temperature and pH steadier than a 20-gallon.

What is the minimum tank for a single adult axolotl?

A single adult axolotl needs at least a 20-gallon long tank, and 30-40 gallons is better. The extra water dilutes waste, which lowers the risk of ammonia spikes and temperature swings. A 40-gallon breeder (36 x 18 x 16 inches) gives the most floor space per adult animal with the most stable chemistry.

What tank size do two axolotls need?

Two adult axolotls need at least a 40-gallon breeder tank, with 60 gallons preferred. The pair needs the extra floor space so each animal holds territory and escapes bites. Keep the two axolotls the same size, because a larger animal bites a smaller one's limbs and gills.

What tank size do baby axolotls need?

A baby axolotl under 3 inches grows best in a 5-10 gallon tank or a small tub with frequent water changes. Babies produce waste fast relative to water volume, so the small setup needs water changed more often than an adult tank. Upgrade the baby as it grows rather than starting it in a huge tank, because large tanks make feeding and cleaning a small animal harder. A 3-6 inch juvenile then moves into the 20-gallon long.

When should I upgrade my axolotl's tank?

Upgrade the tank when the axolotl reaches each size threshold: from tub to 20-gallon at 3-6 inches, and to 30-40 gallons once it passes 6 inches. Plan the upgrade before the animal looks cramped, because crowding causes stress. Buy the adult tank when the animal reaches 6 inches, so the upgrade happens once and the animal never lives in a too-small tank.
