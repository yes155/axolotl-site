How to Acclimate a New Axolotl

Acclimating a new axolotl takes 45-60 minutes and moves the animal into the tank through temperature and water-chemistry steps. Rushing it shocks the gills and skin, spikes stress, and triggers floating or illness. The full method is: float the bag, add tank water gradually, release without the transport water, then leave the animal alone for 24 hours.

Why does acclimation matter?

Acclimation matters because axolotls are sensitive to sudden changes in temperature and water chemistry. A straight dump into the tank shocks the animal, because the bag water and tank water differ in temperature, pH, and hardness. Slow mixing gives the gills and skin time to adjust, which prevents the stress and floating that kill new axolotls. Dumping straight in is a leading cause of new-axolotl loss.

How long does it take to acclimate an axolotl?

Acclimation takes 45-60 minutes total: 15-20 minutes of bag floating for temperature, then gradual water addition before release. A drip method runs 1-2 hours when the bag water and tank water differ a lot in pH or hardness. Do not leave the animal in opened transport water for several hours, because waste ammonia builds up in that small volume and stresses the animal.

How do I acclimate a new axolotl step by step?

Follow these 6 steps in order, and the whole transfer takes about an hour:

  1. Turn off the aquarium lights to reduce stress.
  1. Float the sealed transport bag in the tank for 15-20 minutes to match temperature.
  1. Open the bag and add 1/4 to 1/2 cup of tank water every 5 minutes for 20 minutes.
  1. Net the axolotl out of the bag once the water volume in the bag roughly doubles.
  1. Release the axolotl into the tank.
  1. Discard the transport water - never pour it into the aquarium.

Should I drip acclimate an axolotl?

Yes - use drip acclimation when the bag water and tank water differ significantly in pH, hardness, or temperature. Drip acclimation feeds tank water through airline tubing at a slow steady drip for 1-2 hours. For a local pickup with similar water, bag-floating plus gradual cup additions works fine. Compare the temperatures with a thermometer, and test the pH if the breeder's water differs from yours.

What if my axolotl shipped overnight?

A shipped axolotl that spent over 24 hours in its bag needs rapid acclimation, not a long drip. Float the sealed bag for 15 minutes to equalize temperature, then net the animal straight into the tank. Skip the slow water mixing for shipped animals, because ammonia builds in the transport water and waiting worsens it.

What water conditions do I need before the axolotl arrives?

Before the axolotl arrives, the tank must be fully cycled with ammonia at 0 ppm, nitrite at 0 ppm, and the water at 16-18°C (60-65°F). Dechlorinate the water with a conditioner, because chlorine and chloramine burn axolotl gills. Avoid conditioners that contain aloe, because aloe is toxic to axolotls. Ask the breeder for their water parameters so you can match pH and hardness as closely as possible.

What do I do in the first few days?

Keep the lights off for 24 hours, give the axolotl a hiding spot, and do not handle or feed it on day one. Offer a small meal 24 hours after release, and wait another day if it refuses, because stress suppresses appetite. Watch for curled gills or floating, which mean the animal needs more space and quiet - leave it alone and keep the tank dim.

Should I quarantine a new axolotl?

Yes - quarantine a new axolotl in a separate tub for 2-4 weeks when other axolotls live in the tank. Quarantine stops parasites and disease from reaching the established animals. House the newcomer alone, change its water daily, and watch for fungus or curled gills before moving it into the main tank.
