How to Set Up an Axolotl Tank the Right Way

Set up the aquarium before the axolotl arrives: choose a long tank with enough usable floor area, establish gentle biological filtration, make temperature control reliable, choose safe substrate and hides, and confirm the nitrogen cycle with measured water tests.

Set up and stabilize the aquarium before the axolotl arrives. The sequence is: choose a suitable long tank and stand, plan cooling, install gentle biological filtration, choose safe substrate and hides, fill with treated water, establish the nitrogen cycle, and confirm the system with measured water tests.

This page owns the setup sequence. Exact tank-size decisions, filter comparison, temperature management, water chemistry and cycling interpretation are handled by their specialist pages so this guide does not create competing rules.

Step 1: choose the tank before buying equipment

For one adult, current Petco care guidance recommends a minimum of 20+ gallons and says length is more important than height. MyAxolotl treats a 20-gallon long as the practical lower bound and prefers a 40-gallon breeder as a more forgiving permanent setup when space and budget permit.

That preference is not a claim that one exact gallon number is a universal biological threshold. Use What Size Tank Does an Axolotl Need? for the full reasoning, including babies, juveniles and multiple adults.

Step 2: choose a stable location and stand

Place the aquarium where room temperature is easiest to control and where direct sun, radiators and other heat sources will not create avoidable thermal swings. The stand must be level and rated for the filled aquarium's weight.

If you only know the tank dimensions, use the Aquarium Volume Calculator to estimate water volume and water weight.

Step 3: plan temperature control for your real room

Do not automatically buy a chiller and do not assume a fan will always be enough. Measure the room and tank through the warmest conditions you expect. Amphibian veterinary guidance emphasizes maintaining a species-appropriate stable temperature and notes that chillers or air conditioning may be needed for aquatic systems.

Use Axolotl Tank Temperature for the target and non-purchase cooling methods. If active cooling is required, use Aquarium Chillers and the Chiller Size Calculator.

Step 4: install biological filtration with gentle flow

A conventional home aquarium should use established biological filtration. The filter needs enough colonized media for the actual waste load while returning water gently enough that the axolotl is not continuously pushed around the tank.

Do not choose from a fixed “3×,” “5×,” or “10×” turnover rule. Manufacturer flow ratings do not describe the animal's actual current after media, tubing, spray bars and head loss. Choose by biological capacity, debris capture and controllable return flow.

Use Do Axolotls Need a Filter? and Canister vs Sponge.

Step 5: choose safe substrate

Avoid swallowable gravel and loose stones. Axolotls feed by suction and can ingest material around food. Bare-bottom housing and appropriately fine sand are common approaches, but the correct choice depends on life stage, grain size and maintenance.

Use Choosing the Best Axolotl Substrate. If you are considering gravel, read Gravel Risks first.

Step 6: add hides and safe décor

Provide at least one smooth refuge large enough for the animal to use comfortably. Avoid sharp edges, small holes where the body can become trapped, unstable rockwork and small detachable objects that could be swallowed.

Lighting should support viewing or plants without removing the animal's ability to retreat into shade. See Live vs Artificial Plants for plant/decor choices.

Step 7: use a secure top when escape is possible

A secure aquarium cover is useful where an axolotl could leave the tank or where household pets, objects or contaminants could enter it. Choose a cover that works with the cooling/ventilation plan and equipment rather than sealing a warm tank in a way that traps heat.

Step 8: fill with appropriate treated water

Municipal water may contain chlorine or chloramine. Use a conditioner or water-treatment method appropriate to the disinfectant in your supply. Do not assume that simply letting water stand removes chloramine.

Use Water Conditioners and the Water Conditioner Dosage Calculator for product-volume math.

Step 9: establish the biological filter before adding the axolotl

Do not use the axolotl as the ammonia source for a new uncycled aquarium. Establish the biofilter before the animal arrives. A cycling process uses a controlled ammonia source and water testing to demonstrate that the filter community is processing nitrogenous waste.

Do not declare the tank “cycled” because a certain number of weeks has passed. Confirm the process from measured ammonia, nitrite and nitrate trends. Use Water Parameters & Nitrogen Cycle and log the readings in the Nitrogen Cycle Tracker.

Step 10: learn to test the water correctly

Before the animal arrives, you should be able to measure and record ammonia, nitrite, nitrate, pH and temperature reliably. Clear-looking water can still have unsafe chemistry.

Use How to Test Axolotl Water for sample collection, test-kit technique and color-chart reading.

Step 11: create a maintenance routine before the tank is occupied

Regular testing, waste removal, partial water changes and filter maintenance are part of the setup—not tasks to invent after a problem appears. The 2024 AGSC guide and Merck's 2026 amphibian guidance both support routine partial water replacement in established aquatic systems.

Use How to Change Axolotl Tank Water. Do not replace all mature biological media on a routine calendar.

Step 12: prepare food, transport and acclimation before pickup day

Have the animal's normal food ready and know how the seller has been feeding it. Confirm the transport and arrival plan before purchase, especially for shipped animals.

Use Shipping Live Axolotls and Acclimating a New Axolotl.

What should be ready before the axolotl arrives?

ItemReady when...
Tank and standLevel, secure and sized for the intended animal count.
Temperature controlThe tank remains in the planned range under the warmest expected room conditions.
FilterBiological filtration is established and return flow is gentle.
WaterSource water is treated appropriately and you know how to test it.
Nitrogen cycleReadings demonstrate a functioning biofilter rather than simply an elapsed timeline.
Substrate / décorNo swallowable gravel, sharp edges or trapping hazards.
FoodSuitable food is on hand and portion/frequency are understood.
Emergency planYou know the nearest amphibian/exotics vet and have a temporary safe container if transfer is needed.

Common setup mistakes

  • buying the axolotl before the aquarium is cycled;
  • using a small tank because the animal is currently a juvenile without planning the adult home;
  • choosing a powerful filter that cannot be made gentle;
  • using swallowable gravel;
  • assuming clear water is chemically safe;
  • buying a chiller from tank gallons alone without considering room heat and required pull-down;
  • relying on a fixed cycling timeline rather than test results;
  • making large last-minute changes immediately before the animal arrives; and
  • copying treatment or emergency advice into the setup process instead of keeping the tank stable.
