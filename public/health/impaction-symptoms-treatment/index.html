Axolotl Impaction Symptoms and Treatment

Axolotl impaction is a blockage of the digestive tract, and loose gravel causes the large majority of cases. An impacted axolotl stops eating, grows lethargic, and floats because gas builds behind the blockage. Impaction is preventable and treatable, and the first line of treatment is fasting followed by fridging.

What are the symptoms of axolotl impaction?

The four main symptoms of axolotl impaction are loss of appetite, lethargy, bloating, and floating that cannot be corrected. The bloating appears in the belly, and trapped gas lifts the animal so it struggles to swim down. An impacted axolotl also stops passing waste, because a healthy adult passes feces about once a week and an impacted one passes none.

What causes impaction in axolotls?

Loose gravel is the number-one cause of axolotl impaction, because bottom-feeding axolotls suck in gravel along with their food. Gravel up to 2 cm across lodges in the gut, while fine sand under 2 mm passes through. Oversized food, mouthfuls of plants, and foreign objects in the tank cause the rest of the cases. Whole fish and oversized worms are the main food risks.

How can I tell impaction from constipation?

Impaction and constipation look the same from the outside, so the difference is the cause: a physical blockage versus slowed digestion. Both produce bloating, appetite loss, and floating. Treat the overlap with the same protocol - fasting and fridging - and see a vet if nothing passes within a week. Treat an axolotl that refuses food for several days after eating gravel as impacted.

How do I treat an impacted axolotl?

Treat impaction in four steps: tub the axolotl, stop feeding, fridge if needed, and see a vet if nothing passes.

  1. Move the axolotl to a bare tub with clean, dechlorinated water.
  1. Stop feeding for several days so the gut can clear.
  1. Run a short fridging course at about 5°C (41°F) if nothing passes.
  1. See an exotic vet if waste still does not pass within 1-2 weeks, because some blockages require surgery.

The tub isolates the animal from the gravel source while the gut clears, which is why step 1 matters.

How does fridging help an impacted axolotl?

Fridging slows the axolotl's metabolism, which lets the digestive tract clear a blockage. Set the fridge to about 5°C (41°F) and keep the axolotl in a covered tub of dechlorinated water. Perform a 100% water change daily with pre-chilled water, and run the course for 1-2 weeks or until the blockage passes. Fridge an axolotl only after trying withdrawal of food.

When does impaction need a vet?

An impacted axolotl needs a vet when nothing passes after 1-2 weeks of fasting and fridging, or when the animal shows red skin, gaping, or extreme lethargy. Some blockages require surgical removal, and a vet is the only option for those. An axolotl that cannot right itself needs immediate veterinary care.

How do I prevent impaction?

Prevent impaction with fine sand or a bare-bottom tank, plus food sized to the animal's head. Never use loose gravel in an axolotl tank, because suction feeding pulls it in with every meal. Cut earthworms to bite-size pieces and feed no whole fish, because a floor that cannot be swallowed removes the cause entirely.

Impaction vs Constipation: Know the Difference

Impaction is a physical blockage of the digestive tract — most often by swallowed substrate — and constipation is slow or stalled passage of waste. Both show as a bloated belly and reduced appetite, but impaction is the emergency: a blocked axolotl stops passing waste entirely and can go downhill fast. If you suspect impaction, do not feed, do not heat, and see the treatment steps on this page — then ask a vet.

Which Cause Is Behind the Blockage?

Three causes cover most impactions, and each has its own deep-dive guide:

  • Overfeeding — too much food or too-large prey. Overfeeding and impaction.
  • Substrate — coarse or loose substrate swallowed with food. Substrate guide.
  • Sharp gravel — the classic danger. Gravel risks.
