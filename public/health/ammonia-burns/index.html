How to Treat Axolotl Ammonia Burns

Ammonia burns damage an axolotl's skin, slime coat, and gills within hours, and treating them means removing the ammonia source immediately. Ammonia builds up from waste, uneaten food, and an uncycled filter, and axolotls burn easily because their gills are exposed and their skin carries a delicate slime coat. The recovery outcome depends on how fast the axolotl leaves the toxic water.

What Do Ammonia Burns Look Like?

Ammonia burns show as red or flushed skin, visible red veins, a peeling slime coat, and curled gills. The signs appear first where the skin is thinnest: the belly, legs, and gills.

  • Red or flushed skin on the belly, legs, and gills, which is the classic red or pink burn
  • Red veins visible under the skin, streaking across the surface
  • A peeling or cloudy slime coat, as the protective mucus layer sloughs off
  • Curled, forward-pointing gills and a curled tail tip
  • Gasping at the surface or spending time at the water line, because ammonia irritates the gills
  • Lethargy, lying on the bottom, or a reduced appetite

On dark or heavily pigmented axolotls (melanoid or wild-type), the redness is hard to see, so check the belly, the base of the gills, and the vent where the skin is lighter.

How Severe Are the Burns?

Ammonia burns fall into three severity stages, graded by the ammonia reading and the visible signs. Use this table to judge urgency, because the stage decides whether home care suffices or a vet needs to see the animal.

StageAmmonia levelSignsRecovery outlook
Mild (early)0.25-0.5 ppmSlight redness on gills or belly, gills curling, still active and eating3-7 days with clean water
Moderate0.5-1.0 ppmBrighter red flush, red veins, slime coat peeling, reduced appetite, listless1-6 weeks, watch for infection
SevereAbove 1.0 ppmDeep red or hemorrhaged skin, peeling slime, gasping, lying still or floating, refusing food4-8 weeks; a vet visit is required

Even a moderate burn reverses with fast, correct treatment, and the depth of the damage tracks the time the axolotl spends in the toxic water.

How Do You Treat an Ammonia Burn?

Treat an ammonia burn by moving the axolotl to clean dechlorinated water at the same temperature, then fixing the tank that produced the ammonia. The fastest single action is removing the axolotl from the ammonia source, because the burn stops progressing the moment it sits in clean water.

  1. Move the axolotl to a clean hospital tub immediately, using dechlorinated water at 60-68°F (16-20°C). A tub with 100% fresh, conditioned water removes the ammonia source faster than any chemical.
  1. Treat the source in the main tank: change 50-100% of the water, then dose a conditioner that binds ammonia and nitrite (Seachem Prime) so the remaining traces detoxify. If the tank is not cycled, keep changing water every 12-24 hours until ammonia and nitrite read 0 ppm.
  1. Keep the water cool and clean. Cooler water lowers oxygen demand and skin irritation, so do daily full water changes in the hospital tub while the burn heals.
  1. Do not feed for 24-48 hours. A stressed, burned axolotl needs time to recover before digesting food, and uneaten food in the tub creates more ammonia.
  1. Watch for slime-coat loss. A peeled slime coat leaves the skin open to Saprolegnia (cottony white fungus) and bacteria, so inspect daily and keep conditions pristine.
  1. Use topical treatments only with veterinary guidance. A black tea bath soothes slime-coat recovery, and a salt bath treats fungus, but both stress an already stressed axolotl, so reserve them for confirmed fungus.

Why Does Ammonia Reappear After Water Changes?

Ammonia reappears when the water conditioner does not remove chloramine, which many municipal supplies use instead of chlorine. Standard conditioners that remove only chlorine leave chloramine intact, and the tank then keeps reading ammonia. Check the conditioner label for chloramine removal before filling the tub or tank.

When Does an Ammonia Burn Need a Vet?

See a vet when the axolotl refuses food for 3 days, floats or gasps continuously, shows red hemorrhaging under the skin, grows fuzzy white patches on the burn, or shows no improvement within 1 week of clean water. A stage-3 burn (ammonia above 1.0 ppm) merits an exam even when the axolotl looks stable, because gill and slime-coat damage inside the body stays invisible from outside.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take an axolotl to recover from ammonia burns?

Mild burns heal in 3-7 days of pristine water, moderate burns take 1-6 weeks with careful monitoring, and severe burns take 4-8 weeks with veterinary support.

Do ammonia burns go away on their own?

Yes, ammonia burns resolve in clean water, and the redness fades while the slime coat regenerates. The completeness of recovery tracks the severity and the speed of the response, so fix the water the day the burn develops.

Can axolotls survive high ammonia?

Axolotls survive ammonia spikes when the exposure is brief and the water is fixed fast, and they die from prolonged high ammonia. A reading above 1.0 ppm for hours causes tissue damage, so act immediately at the first sign of redness.

Do ammonia burns turn axolotls red?

Yes, ammonia irritation flushes the skin red, most obviously on the belly, legs, and gill bases, and red veins appear under the skin. The redness fades within 3-7 days once the axolotl sits in clean water.

How do you fix ammonia in an axolotl tank?

Fix tank ammonia by removing the source (waste and uneaten food), changing 50-100% of the water, dosing a conditioner that detoxifies ammonia and nitrite, and completing the nitrogen cycle. Until the cycle matures, test daily and change water every 12-24 hours so ammonia and nitrite stay at 0 ppm.
