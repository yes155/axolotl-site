Setting Up an Axolotl Hospital Quarantine Tub

A bare plastic tub with daily 100% water changes is the most effective hospital setup for a sick, new, or injured axolotl, because it gives total control over water quality. No filter, no gravel, and no chemicals are needed, because fresh dechlorinated water replaces them every day. The tub removes nearly every variable that makes an axolotl sick in the first place.

When Do You Need a Quarantine Tub?

Set up a quarantine tub any time you need full control over water quality to protect both the axolotl and the main tank. That covers new arrivals, sick or injured axolotls, skin or gill problems, healing wounds, recovery after treatment, and animals being medicated.

  • A new axolotl: quarantine every newcomer for at least 30 days before it joins the main tank
  • A sick or injured axolotl: isolate it for closer monitoring and safer water control
  • Skin, gill, or wound problems: a clean tub stops fungus and infection from worsening
  • Bath treatment: salt baths and black tea baths run in separate containers, then the axolotl returns to its tub
  • After fridging or shipping: a calm, controlled tub supports recovery

What Do You Need for a Quarantine Tub?

A quarantine tub needs seven items: a plastic tub, a vented lid, water conditioner, a thermometer, a siphon or turkey baster, a hide, and a second identical tub for daily swaps. Use a 6-quart tub for axolotls under 6 inches and a 10-12 quart tub for larger adults, and make sure the plastic is food-grade and crack-free.

  • A plastic storage tub: 6 quarts for axolotls under 6 inches, 10-12 quarts for larger adults, food-grade plastic, crack-free
  • A lid with holes: drilled or punched ventilation keeps the water cool and keeps the axolotl in
  • Water conditioner: a brand that removes chlorine and chloramine and binds ammonia, such as Seachem Prime
  • A thermometer: the tub water matches the main tank at 60-68°F (16-20°C)
  • A siphon or turkey baster: for daily water changes and removing leftovers
  • A hide: one smooth PVC pipe or small plastic cave, with nothing rough or sharp
  • A second identical tub: so one tub holds the axolotl while the other is cleaned and refilled for the next day

How Do You Set Up a Quarantine Tub?

Set up the tub with hot-water-only cleaning, dechlorinated temperature-matched water, a bare bottom, one hide, and no filter, then cover it with the vented lid. Six steps produce a working hospital in under ten minutes.

  1. Clean the tub with hot water only. Soap and chemical disinfectants leave residue that irritates skin; a vinegar rinse is fine when followed by a thorough fresh-water rinse.
  1. Fill the tub with dechlorinated water at the same temperature as the water the axolotl came from, to avoid temperature shock. Treat the water with conditioner before adding the axolotl.
  1. Keep the bottom bare. Gravel and sand trap waste, and a sick axolotl with peeling slime must not rest on abrasive surfaces.
  1. Add one hide and nothing else. Keep the tub in a dim, quiet, cool spot away from direct light and loud vibration.
  1. Skip the filter. Because the water changes 100% daily, a filter is unnecessary, and a filter adds surfaces that harbor bacteria.
  1. Cover the tub with the vented lid. A startled axolotl in a shallow tub jumps out overnight.

What Is the Daily Care Routine?

Change 100% of the tub water every day, because ammonia builds fast in a small volume of water. The daily routine takes about 10 minutes.

  1. Change 100% of the water with fresh, dechlorinated water matched to the tub temperature.
  1. Wipe waste from the bottom and rinse the hide with hot water.
  1. Dose the conditioner for the new water according to the bottle.
  1. Check the temperature once or twice daily.
  1. Observe the axolotl and note appetite, posture, gill curl, wounds, and colors, because a written record reveals trends.
  1. Feed small, easy meals only when the axolotl eats, and remove anything uneaten within 15-20 minutes.

How Long Does Quarantine Last?

Quarantine new axolotls for at least 30 days, and keep a sick axolotl tubbed until it eats well, improves visibly, and heals its wounds. The 30-day window covers the incubation period of the common problems that arrive with a new animal. Return a sick axolotl to the main tank only after that tank's water verifies clean.

What Do You Never Do in a Quarantine Tub?

Never medicate without a diagnosis, never add salt unless prescribed, never skip the daily water change, and never put the tub in a hot room or direct sunlight. A clean tub plus pristine water cures most minor problems by itself, so adding products makes matters worse.

  • Do not medicate without a diagnosis, because baths and antibiotics carry real risks
  • Do not use salt unless a vet prescribes it, because salt irritates damaged skin
  • Do not skip daily water changes, because ammonia builds fast in a small tub
  • Do not place the tub in a hot room or direct sunlight, because the water warms past 68°F (20°C)

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a quarantine tank replace a quarantine tub?

Yes, a small bare glass tank works as a quarantine tank, and the requirements are identical: bare bottom, no filter, daily 100% water changes. A plastic tub is cheaper, lighter, and easier to store, so keepers prefer it.

How big should an axolotl quarantine tub be?

Use a 6-quart tub for axolotls under 6 inches and a 10-12 quart tub for adults. The tub must also let the axolotl stretch out straight and turn around, so err toward larger.

Do axolotls need a filter in a quarantine tub?

No, axolotls in a quarantine tub need no filter, because daily 100% water changes keep the water clean and no filter exists to fail or hide bacteria.

How long do you quarantine a new axolotl?

Quarantine a new axolotl for at least 30 days, watching eating, skin, gill, and behavior for problems before it joins the main tank.

Can you use tap water in a quarantine tub?

Yes, tap water works in a quarantine tub when a conditioner removes both chlorine and chloramine, and the water is temperature-matched to the axolotl's current water.
